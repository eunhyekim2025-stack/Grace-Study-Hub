# Module 6 — Cost-Volume-Profit (CVP) Analysis

> [[management-accounting/index|← Management Accounting]] · Previous: [[m05-cost-behaviour-estimation|Module 5]] · Next: [[m07-variable-vs-absorption-costing|Module 7 — Variable vs Absorption]]

**CVP = Cost · Volume · Profit.** The most practical tool in the whole course.

---

## Contribution margin format

| Item | Total | Per unit | Percent |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Sales | £240,000 | £30 | 100% |
| Less: Variable Expenses | (£144,000) | (£18) | 60% |
| **Contribution Margin** | **£96,000** | **£12** | **40%** |
| Less: Fixed Expenses | (£60,000) | – | – |
| **Operating Income** | **£36,000** | – | – |

**What a 40% CM ratio means:** every extra £100 of sales adds £40 of profit (once fixed costs are covered).

---

## Essential CVP formulas

**Break-even in units**

$$\text{BEP (units)} = \frac{\text{Fixed Expenses}}{\text{CM per unit}} = \frac{60{,}000}{12} = 5{,}000$$

**Break-even in pounds**

$$\text{BEP (£)} = \frac{\text{Fixed Expenses}}{\text{CM Ratio}} = \frac{60{,}000}{0.40} = £150{,}000$$

**Units required for a target profit**

$$\text{Units} = \frac{\text{Fixed Expenses} + \text{Target Operating Income}}{\text{CM per unit}}$$

**Target operating income when there are taxes**

$$\text{Target Operating Income} = \frac{\text{Net Income}}{1 - \text{Tax Rate}}$$

> [!warning] A favourite exam trap
> For a **target net income of £300,000 with a 25% tax rate**,
> Target Operating Income = £300,000 ÷ 0.75 = **£400,000**.
> That £400,000 is what goes into the units formula above. (Check: 400,000 × 0.75 = 300,000 ✓)

---

## Risk measures

### Margin of Safety

**How much sales can drop before the firm hits break-even.**

- £240,000 − £150,000 = **£90,000**, i.e. **37.5%** of current sales
- The larger it is, the safer the business. A thin margin of safety means a mild downturn pushes you into losses.

### Operating Leverage

$$\text{Degree of Operating Leverage} = \frac{\text{CM}}{\text{Operating Income}} = \frac{96{,}000}{36{,}000} \approx 2.67$$

**Higher fixed costs increase leverage, meaning profits swing more wildly with sales changes.**

| | High fixed cost (e.g. a chip fab) | High variable cost (e.g. distribution, outsourced) |
|---|---|---|
| Leverage | High | Low |
| In a boom | Profit explodes | Profit rises gently |
| In a downturn | Risk of heavy losses | Rides it out |

> [!important] Common misreading
> High leverage does **not** mean high profitability. It means **high amplitude — more risk.**

## Multi-product CVP — the sales mix

When products have different contribution margins, "the break-even quantity" becomes ambiguous.
Calculate a **Weighted Average CM** based on the **Sales Mix** and treat the whole business as one blended product.

| Product | Sales mix | CM per unit (assumed) | Weighted contribution |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Enthusiast | 60% | £10 | £6.0 |
| Jammer | 35% | £20 | £7.0 |
| Pro | 5% | £60 | £3.0 |
| **Weighted average CM per unit** | | | **£16.0** |

Now divide fixed costs by £16 for the total break-even quantity, then split it back out 60 / 35 / 5.

*(The mix percentages come from the source guide; the per-unit CM figures are assumed here to show the calculation.)*

> [!tip] The hidden trap
> **If the sales mix shifts, the break-even point moves even when total volume does not.**
> Sell more of the low-margin product and the weighted average CM falls, so you need more units just to stand still.
> This is why *what* you sell matters more than *how much*.
