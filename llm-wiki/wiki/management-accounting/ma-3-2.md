> [!summary] Key takeaways  
- CM drives profit; CM ratio shows the % of each sales dollar that contributes to covering fixed costs.  
- Use either the full‑equation or incremental method—both give identical results if steps are shown.  
- NOI = Total CM – Fixed Expenses; adjust NOI by analyzing fixed‑cost changes, CM/unit changes, and volume changes.  
- Target profit = Fixed + Desired profit; required CM per unit = (Fixed + Profit) ÷ Units.  
- Degree of Operating Leverage = Total CM ÷ NOI, measuring profit sensitivity to sales volume.

## Contribution Margin Basics
> [!info] **Contribution Margin (CM)**  
- CM = Sales – Variable Cost; measures profit available to cover fixed costs.  

> [!info] **CM Ratio**  
- CM ratio = CM ÷ Sales (e.g., 40.5 %).  

## CVP Analysis Methods
> [!info] **Full‑equation method** – write profit = (CM per unit × Q) – Fixed and solve for the unknown.  
> [!info] **Incremental (differential) method** – compute ΔCM and ΔFixed, then combine.  

## Incremental Impact Example
> [!example]  
- 48 extra units × $200 CM/unit = **+$8,000** ΔCM.  
- Fixed expenses rise **+$10,000**.  
- Net impact on NOI = **‑$2,000** (negative).  

## NOI Calculation Example
> [!example]  
- Sales: 500 units × $575 = $287,500.  
- Expenses: Fixed $111,125 + Variable $74,000 = $185,125.  
- **NOI = $32,375**.  

> [!warning] Reducing fixed expenses raises NOI dollar‑for‑dollar; increasing variable cost per unit lowers CM/unit and NOI.

## Three‑Step Impact Analysis for Volume Change (500 → 575 units)
| Step | Effect | NOI impact |
|------|--------|------------|
| 1 | Fixed‑expense reduction | +$2,000 |
| 2 | CM/unit change (e.g., $15 → $20) on original 500 units | –$? (depends on data) |
| 3 | Additional 75 units × $185 CM/unit | **+$13,875** |
| **Total** | | ≈ **+$12,000** (matches full‑equation result) |

## Target Profit Calculations
> [!info] **Per‑unit CM needed for a profit increase**  
- Desired profit $3,000 over 150 bikes → $3,000 ÷ 150 = **$20** extra CM per bike.  

> [!example]  
- One‑time $300 machine sale → $300 ÷ 150 = $2 per bike; remaining $18 must come from price increase or cost reduction.  

> [!info] **Sales‑mix allocation (RM example)**  
- Fixed expense rises to RM 125 k; target NOI ≈ RM 50 k → Target CM = RM 175 k.  
- Hardcover mix = 62.5 % → allocate 62.5 % of required sales to hardcover; remaining 37.5 % to other lines.  

## Degree of Operating Leverage (DOL)
> [!info] **DOL formula** – DOL = Total CM ÷ NOI.  
- Higher DOL → profit more sensitive to sales‑volume changes.

## Cost‑Increase Scenario
> [!example]  
- Variable cost ↑ 10 %; Fixed cost (e.g., railroad) ↑ 20 % → new total fixed = $260,000 × 1.20 = **$476,000**.  
- Re‑compute CM per unit and NOI with same volume (e.g., 334 units).  

## Break‑Even & Profit‑Target Analysis
> [!info] **Break‑Even** – set profit = 0, solve for sales.  
> [!info] **Profit‑Target** – set profit = desired amount, solve for required sales:  
\[
\text{Required Sales} = \frac{\text{Fixed} + \text{Target Profit}}{\text{CM Ratio}}
\]

## Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Contribution Margin (CM) | Sales minus variable cost; profit available for fixed costs. |
| CM Ratio | CM ÷ Sales, expressed as a percentage. |
| Net Operating Income (NOI) | Total CM minus fixed expenses. |
| Degree of Operating Leverage (DOL) | Total CM ÷ NOI; measures profit sensitivity to volume. |
| Break‑Even Point | Sales level where profit = 0. |
| Incremental Analysis | Evaluates profit impact of changes using ΔCM and ΔFixed. |
| Fixed Expense | Costs that do not vary with production volume. |
| Variable Cost per Unit | Cost that varies directly with each unit produced. |
