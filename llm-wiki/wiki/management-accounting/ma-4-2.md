> [!summary] Key takeaways
- POHR = Budgeted overhead ÷ activity driver; denominator can be estimated activity or full capacity.  
- Capacity‑based POHR stabilizes product cost and isolates idle‑capacity expense as a period cost.  
- Job‑order costing applies overhead via POHR; compare applied vs actual to adjust COGS.  
- Under‑applied overhead increases COGS; over‑applied reduces it.  
- Unused capacity cost is reported separately, aiding capacity‑utilization decisions.

## 1. Manufacturing Overhead & POHR

> [!info] Manufacturing overhead  
All indirect production costs (e.g., indirect labor, utilities, depreciation).

> [!info] Predetermined overhead rate (POHR)  
POHR = Budgeted manufacturing overhead ÷ Budgeted activity level (cost driver).

### 1.1 Traditional (estimated activity) method
- Denominator = activity expected for the period (units, machine‑hours, labor‑hours).  
- Product cost fluctuates if actual volume differs from estimate.

### 1.2 Capacity (full‑capacity) method
- Denominator = maximum possible output at full capacity.  
- Keeps POHR constant; idle capacity cost is shown separately.

#### Comparison

| Method | Denominator | POHR (example) | Product cost per unit | Treatment of idle capacity |
|--------|-------------|----------------|-----------------------|----------------------------|
| Traditional | Estimated activity (40,000 units) | $2.50/unit | $24 + $2.50 = $26.50 | Implicitly included in product cost |
| Capacity | Full capacity (50,000 units) | $2.00/unit | $24 + $2.00 = $26.00 | Recorded as period expense ($2.00 × idle units) |

> [!warning] Using an unchanged estimator can distort product cost when actual volume changes.

### 1.3 Steps to compute capacity‑based POHR
1. Identify total budgeted manufacturing overhead.  
2. Determine full production capacity (units, hours, etc.).  
3. Divide overhead by capacity → POHR per unit of capacity.

### 1.4 Income‑statement impact
- Traditional format: only product‑related costs appear in COGS.  
- Capacity format: adds a line “Cost of unused capacity” (period cost), moving idle‑capacity expense out of product cost.

## 2. Job‑Order Costing & Overhead Application

> [!info] Predetermined overhead rate for the example  
Budgeted overhead $300 ÷ Budgeted direct‑labor hours 15 = **$20 per DLH**.

### 2.1 Apply overhead to each job
- Job 10: 28 DLH × $20 = **$560**  
- Job 11: 42 DLH × $20 = **$840**  
- Total applied OH = **$1,400**.

> [!example] Total direct‑labor hours verification  
Actual DLH = 75 hrs (derived from 1,050 ÷ 15).

### 2.2 Compare applied vs actual overhead
- Actual overhead incurred = **$1,480**.  
- Under‑applied overhead = $1,480 – $1,400 = **$80**.

> [!warning] Under‑applied overhead must be added to COGS; over‑applied would be subtracted.

### 2.3 Adjust COGS
- Unadjusted COGS = $3,850.  
- Adjusted COGS = $3,850 + $80 = **$3,930** (example shows $3,900; adjust per problem data).

> [!info] Job‑costing steps  
1. Compute POHR.  
2. Multiply each job’s DLH by POHR → applied OH.  
3. Sum applied OH for period.  
4. Compare to actual OH → under/over‑applied amount.  
5. Adjust COGS accordingly.

## 3. Managerial Implications

- Capacity‑based POHR highlights idle‑capacity cost, supporting decisions on expansion, new products, or outsourcing.  
- Traditional POHR may mislead cost‑volume‑profit analysis when production deviates from estimates.  
- Accurate overhead adjustment ensures COGS reflects true manufacturing cost.

## Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Manufacturing overhead | All indirect costs of production (e.g., indirect labor, utilities). |
| Predetermined overhead rate (POHR) | Budgeted overhead divided by a chosen activity driver. |
| Traditional POHR | Uses estimated activity as denominator. |
| Capacity POHR | Uses full‑capacity level as denominator; idle capacity shown as period cost. |
| Under‑applied overhead | Actual overhead > applied overhead; added to COGS. |
| Over‑applied overhead | Applied overhead > actual overhead; subtracted from COGS. |
| Cost of unused capacity | Expense of idle capacity recorded separately from product cost. |
