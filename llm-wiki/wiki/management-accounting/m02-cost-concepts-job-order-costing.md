# Module 2 — Cost Concepts & Job Order Costing

## The product cost trinity — the root of every module

From Module 1 through 12, every calculation comes back to identifying these three.

| Component | Classification | Burger-shop example |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct Materials (DM)** | Product · variable | The meat, cheese, tomato and bun |
| **Direct Labour (DL)** | Product · variable | The chef's wages for the minutes spent cooking this burger |
| **Manufacturing Overhead (MO)** | Product · mixed/fixed | Gas for the grill, lighting, cleaning supplies, factory rent |

**Direct vs indirect turns on one thing: can you trace it?**
"100g of beef went into this burger" — countable, so direct.
"How much electricity did this burger use?" — not countable, so indirect.

> [!important] The whole difficulty of this course lives in MO
> DM and DL are just counting. The real question is always **how much of MO to assign to whom**,
> and because there is no single correct answer, three modules exist to attack it:
> [[m02-cost-concepts-job-order-costing|Module 2 (POHR)]] →
> [[m03-process-costing|Module 3 (equivalent units)]] →
> [[m04-activity-based-costing|Module 4 (ABC)]].

### Two useful pairings

- **Prime Cost = DM + DL** — only the directly traceable costs
- **Conversion Cost = DL + MO** — what it takes to *convert* materials into a product. The star of Module 3.

---

## Product costs vs period costs

- **Product costs (factory)** — incurred inside the production facility: DM, DL, MO
- **Period costs (outside the factory)** — administration, selling and R&D: CEO salary, patent lawyers, advertising

What actually differs is **when the cost becomes an expense**. A product cost sits in inventory until the
unit sells; a period cost hits the income statement this month. That timing gap is the seed of the
[[m07-variable-vs-absorption-costing|overproduction trap in Module 7]].

### Classification traps

| Item | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Factory supervisor's salary | Product cost (MO) | Inside the wall, but not traceable to a unit → indirect |
| CEO salary | Period cost | Head office — outside the wall |
| Factory rent | Product cost (MO) | A fixed cost inside the wall |
| Head-office rent | Period cost | Same word "rent", different location, different class |
| Sales commission | Period cost | Variable, but selling is not manufacturing |
| Factory cleaning supplies | Product cost (MO) | Needed for production, not traceable to a unit |

## How cost travels through inventory

```
DM ─┐
DL ─┼→ Work in Process ──complete──→ Finished Goods ──sold──→ COGS
MO ─┘        (WIP)                        (FG)              (an expense at last)

Period costs ───────────────────────────────────────────────→ Income statement (skips the warehouses)
```

All three stores obey the same equation — which is also what a Module 3 production report really is.

> **Beginning inventory + costs added this period − ending inventory = cost that left this period**

| Store | Beginning + added | − Ending | = What left |
|---|---|---|---|
| Raw Materials | Beginning RM + purchases | Ending RM | Materials used in production |
| Work in Process | Beginning WIP + (DM+DL+MO) | Ending WIP | **Cost of goods manufactured** |
| Finished Goods | Beginning FG + cost of goods manufactured | Ending FG | **Cost of goods sold (COGS)** |

---

## The Predetermined Overhead Rate (POHR)

To apply costs before the actual utility bills or taxes arrive, we use an estimate:

$$\text{POHR} = \frac{\text{Estimated Total Manufacturing Overhead}}{\text{Estimated Total Allocation Base (e.g. Direct Labour Hours)}}$$

**Worked through** — suppose at the start of the year you estimate MO of £100,000 and 5,000 direct labour hours.

- POHR = 100,000 ÷ 5,000 = **£20 per direct labour hour**
- A job that consumed 3 labour hours is charged 3 × 20 = **£60 of overhead**
- No waiting for the electricity bill.

## Job order costing vs process costing

| | Job Order Costing | Process Costing |
|---|---|---|
| Used when | Every unit is unique / customised | Units are mass-produced and identical |
| Examples | Architectural design, car repairs, custom wedding cakes | Yoga mats, gasoline, crayons |
| Cost container | A single job | One period of a process |

> [!tip] One question tells them apart
> **"Can a customer point at it and call it *mine*?"**
> A custom wedding cake is somebody's cake → job order costing.
> A litre of petrol at a pump has no owner worth distinguishing → process costing.
