# Module 8 — Budgeting

> [[management-accounting/index|← Management Accounting]] · Previous: [[m07-variable-vs-absorption-costing|Module 7]] · Next: [[m09-12-performance-management|Modules 9–12]]

Planning is formalised through the **Master Budget**.

---

## Three tiers

### 1. The Sales Budget

**The absolute starting point and "north star."**
All other budgets — production, materials, labour — flow from the sales forecast.

> [!warning] Which is exactly the risk
> If the sales forecast is wrong, **every budget beneath it is wrong too**, in a chain.
> This is where the effort in budgeting belongs.

### 2. Operating Budgets

Production, materials purchasing, labour, manufacturing overhead and SG&A.
Together these lead to the creation of the **Budgeted Income Statement**.

### 3. Financial Budgets

These culminate in the **Cash Budget** and the **Budgeted Balance Sheet**.

> [!tip] Why cash gets its own budget
> A profitable company still dies if it runs out of cash. You can show a surplus on the budgeted income
> statement and still fail to make payroll because receipts arrive late. So **when money moves in and out**
> is tracked separately from whether you made a profit.

---

## Where the curriculum goes next

The logical progression continues into **Variance Analysis** (comparing actuals to these budgets) and
**Responsibility Accounting** (evaluating managers based on their specific segments).
