> [!summary] Key takeaways  
- Separate **relevant** from **irrelevant** data before any calculation.  
- Special‑order decisions hinge on **incremental contribution** (price – variable cost) × quantity, provided idle capacity exists.  
- For sell‑or‑process choices, ignore joint costs; compare **incremental benefit** with **incremental cost**.  
- Capacity limits and opportunity costs can turn a seemingly positive contribution into a loss.  

## 1. Problem‑analysis checklist
- List **all** given information.  
- Label each item **relevant** (affects decision) or **irrelevant** (does not).  
- Distinguish **given** data from values that must be **derived**.  

> [!info]  
**Relevant data** : machine cost $100 (no increase), fixed monthly cost $12,000, shipping cost >$13,000 (only if it impacts the decision).  

> [!warning]  
**Irrelevant data** : overall company revenue, unrelated overhead – they do not change the specific outcome.

## 2. Special‑order analysis (managerial accounting)

> [!info]  
**Definition** – A one‑time request that does **not** alter the firm’s regular production mix or long‑term strategy.

### General decision steps
| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Verify idle capacity (order must fit within unused capacity). |
| 2 | Exclude all fixed costs that will be incurred regardless of the order. |
| 3 | Identify the **incremental selling price** offered. |
| 4 | Determine the **incremental variable cost** per unit. |
| 5 | Compute **incremental contribution** = (price – variable cost) × quantity. |
| 6 | Accept if contribution > 0 **and** capacity constraints are satisfied. |

### Example: JetCorp
> [!example]  
- Regular price: $20/unit  
- Special order: 3,000 units @ $10/unit  
- Capacity: 10,000 units; current use 5,000 → 5,000 idle (enough).  
- Variable manufacturing cost: $8/unit (direct materials, labor, variable overhead).  
- Incremental contribution per unit = $10 – $8 = **$2**.  
- Total incremental contribution = 3,000 × $2 = **$6,000** → **Accept**.

### Hotel‑room special‑order
- Request: 20 single, 15 deluxe, 1 suite.  
- Occupancy: 80 % of 100 % capacity → only **5** spare rooms.  
- Accepting would displace higher‑margin bookings → must weigh **opportunity cost** of lost regular revenue.

## 3. Incremental profit calculations (room revenue & cost)

| Room type | Qty | Incremental revenue | Incremental cost per unit | Total incremental cost | Incremental profit |
|-----------|-----|--------------------|---------------------------|------------------------|-------------------|
| Single    | 20  | 20 × $150 = $3,000 | $50 + $20 = $70           | 20 × $70 = $1,400      | $3,000 – $1,400 = **$1,600** |
| Deluxe    | 5   | 5 × $200 = $1,000  | $80 + $30 = $110          | 5 × $110 = $550        | $1,000 – $550 = **$450** |

> [!info]  
**Price‑difference opportunity** – A drop from $250 to $200 creates a $50 gap; for 10 rooms, extra revenue = 10 × $50 = **$500**.

## 4. Sell‑or‑process (joint‑cost) decisions

> [!info]  
**Joint (common) costs** are **irrelevant** for the sell‑or‑process choice; only **incremental processing cost** and **incremental benefit** matter.

### Decision rule
> [!info]  
- **If** incremental benefit > incremental cost → **process further**.  
- **If** incremental cost > incremental benefit → **sell as‑is**.

### Illustrative examples
| Product | Incremental benefit | Incremental cost | Decision |
|---------|--------------------|------------------|----------|
| Wood (final) | $470 – $140 = $330 | $130 | **Process** |
| Sawdust | $50 – $40 = $10 | $20 | **Do not process** |
| Federal benefit (kg) | 8.5 kg × ($50 – $2) = $408 | 8.5 kg × $20 = $170 | **Process** |

> [!warning]  
Allocating joint costs to products (for reporting) does **not** affect the economic decision; ignore these allocations in the analysis.

## 5. Additional cost‑comparison illustration
- Base price = $20.40.  
- Alternative price = x + $2.  
- Set equal: $20.40 = x + $2 → **x = $18.40** (maximum acceptable price).

## 6. Decision framework summary
1. **Identify** all data → label relevant/irrelevant.  
2. **Special order?** Apply incremental contribution steps.  
3. **Sell vs. process?** Compare incremental benefit vs. incremental cost, ignoring joint costs.  
4. **Check capacity & opportunity costs** before finalizing.

## Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Relevant cost | Cost that changes as a result of the decision; typically incremental or variable. |
| Irrelevant cost | Cost that remains unchanged regardless of the decision (e.g., sunk, fixed within relevant range). |
| Incremental contribution | (Special‑order price – variable cost) × quantity. |
| Opportunity cost | Benefit foregone by allocating resources to the special order instead of the next best alternative. |
| Joint cost | Cost incurred up to a split‑off point; irrelevant for sell‑or‑process decisions. |
| Incremental benefit | Additional revenue obtained from processing further or accepting a special order. |
