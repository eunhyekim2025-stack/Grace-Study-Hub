# Seminar 1 — Costs for Decision Making

> [[index|← Management accounting]] · Closing section of Seminar 1, from the class recording. The full reference treatment lives in [[m02-cost-concepts-job-order-costing]] — this note keeps what the *class* added: the lecturer's framing, the worked quick-checks, and his marking guidance.

---

> [!summary] Key takeaways
> - Relevance is decided **per decision**, not per cost. The same dollar figure can be relevant in one choice and irrelevant in the next.
> - **Opportunity cost** is invisible in the accounting records — it never happened, so nothing recorded it. It can be **non-financial** (time, friendship).
> - **Differential (incremental) cost** is always relevant, because it *is* the difference between the alternatives.
> - **Sunk cost** is always irrelevant. But the **resale value today** of the same asset is not sunk — that is a live, relevant amount.
> - Marking: any **logical** method is accepted. Workings are what make partial credit possible.

## 1 · The frame

Decision making means choosing between alternatives. So for every cost — and every *benefit* — ask one question:

> Does this differ depending on which alternative I choose?

- **Yes** → relevant cost / relevant benefit → include it.
- **No** → irrelevant → leave it out entirely.

> [!info] Both directions matter
> The lecturer was explicit that this applies to **benefits** as much as costs. A revenue that is identical under both options is just as irrelevant as a cost that is.

## 2 · Opportunity cost

The **potential benefit given up** when you choose one alternative over another.

> [!warning] Why you will never find it in the books
> "When you look at your financial records in a company, you cannot find this anywhere — because it is a cost that you gave up. You never received the benefit itself, so it doesn't exist in history."
>
> Nothing was paid, so nothing was recorded. Opportunity cost has to be reasoned in by hand every time. This is exactly why it is easy to forget in an exam.

**It does not have to be money.** The class example:

| Alternative | What you get | What you give up |
|---|---|---|
| Work the weekend | ~$50 earned | time with friends — bonding, friendship |
| Go out with friends | bonding, friendship | ~$50 earned |

Whichever you pick, the other column is your opportunity cost. Choosing friends makes the forgone $50 an opportunity cost; choosing work makes the forgone friendship one — **a non-financial benefit is still a real cost**.

## 3 · Differential (incremental) cost and revenue

The difference in cost — or revenue — between two alternatives. **Always relevant**, because the difference between the alternatives is precisely what a decision turns on.

> [!example] The upsize
> A meal costs **$10**. Upsizing costs **$1.20** more.
>
> The decision is *"upsize or not"*, so the only figure in play is the **$1.20** differential and whatever extra satisfaction it buys. The **$10** is identical under both alternatives — it is not part of this decision at all.

## 4 · Sunk cost

A cost **already incurred**, which no present or future choice can change. **Always irrelevant.**

> [!example] The phone
> A new phone costs **$900**. Your old phone cost **$700** two years ago. Its resale value today is **$520**.
>
> | Figure | Verdict | Why |
> |---|---|---|
> | $700 paid for the old phone | **Sunk — ignore** | Already spent; buying or not buying changes nothing about it |
> | $520 resale value today | **Relevant** | You only receive it under one alternative — it is a live differential benefit |
> | $900 price of the new phone | **Relevant** | Incurred only if you buy |

> [!warning] The trap
> The $700 and the $520 attach to the *same object*, which is what makes this confusing. They are different amounts serving different roles: one is history, the other is a choice you still have. Same item, different costs and benefits altogether.

## 5 · Quick checks from class — drive or take the train?

The decision throughout: **drive to the destination, or take the train?**

| # | Item | Answer | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cost of the **train ticket** | **Relevant** | Incurred only if you take the train; driving avoids it entirely. It differs between alternatives |
| 2 | Annual **car licensing fee** | **Not relevant** | You pay it whether you drive this trip or not. It does not differ between the alternatives — it is sunk relative to this decision |
| 3 | The car's **resale value**, if you could sell it | **Relevant** | If selling is genuinely available, the amount is capturable and becomes part of the decision — so it is *not* sunk |

> [!info] What #3 turns on
> The lecturer's point: whether something is sunk depends on whether the money is still *reachable*. A car bought years ago for $200,000 — that purchase price is sunk. But the $5,000 you could get for it today is not, provided selling is actually an option in this decision.

## 6 · Exam technique — the lecturer's own instructions

Said twice, unprompted, so treat it as a marking policy rather than an aside.

> [!tip] Method
> "As long as your method is **logical and makes sense**, it is accepted. You might do a long-winded method, but if it makes sense, it's fine." There are several routes to an answer, and it does **not** have to be the one demonstrated in class.

> [!warning] Workings
> "Workings are for you to help me to help you."
>
> - Right answer by a shortcut → fine, no problem.
> - **Wrong** answer with no visible method → **no marks are possible**, because there is nothing to give credit to.
> - Wrong answer with clear workings → the marker can award whatever the method earns.
>
> The same goes for legibility: a method he cannot read is a method he cannot mark.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Relevant cost / benefit** | Differs between the alternatives and still lies in the future — include it |
| **Irrelevant cost** | Identical under every alternative — exclude it entirely |
| **Opportunity cost** | The benefit forgone by not choosing the other alternative; never recorded, may be non-financial |
| **Differential (incremental) cost** | The difference in cost between two alternatives — relevant by definition |
| **Differential revenue** | The difference in revenue between two alternatives — likewise always relevant |
| **Sunk cost** | Already incurred and unchangeable — always irrelevant, however large |
