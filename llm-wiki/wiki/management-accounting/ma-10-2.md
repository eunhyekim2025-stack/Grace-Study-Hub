> [!summary] Key takeaways
> - Unit product cost = $4.99 (DM $2.00 + DL $2.48 + MOH $0.51).  
> - Master budget flows from sales forecast → production costs → COGS/SGA → NOI → cash budget.  
> - Cash budget triggers financing only if ending cash < $30,000.  
> - Service‑industry budgets replace material/production budgets with labor‑driven cost drivers.  
> - Balance‑sheet items are linked directly to their respective sub‑budgets.

## Cost allocation & unit product cost
- PLHR (per‑labor‑hour rate) = **$49.703** → Direct overhead rate (DOHR) = $49.703 per DRB.  
- Direct‑labor required per unit = **0.05 DRB** → labor cost per unit = $2.48.  
- Direct‑material cost: 5 lb × $0.40/lb = **$2.00**.  
- Manufacturing overhead (MOH) applied via DOHR → **≈ $0.51** per unit.  
- Total unit product cost ≈ **$4.99** (rounded).

> [!info] **Unit product cost formula**  
> Unit Cost = Direct Material + Direct Labor + Applied Manufacturing Overhead.

## Pricing, revenue target & SMA
- Desired final‑delivery revenue = **$5,000**.  
- With unit cost $4.99, required sales volume = 5,000 ÷ 4.99 ≈ **1,002 units**.  
- Sales‑margin allowance (SMA) = **$0.50/unit**; typical monthly volume = **70 k units** → SMA cost $10‑$20 k.  
- Base price component = $0.20/unit → total price contribution ≈ $10 k for the month.

## Cash budget rules
- Minimum cash balance policy: **$30,000**.  
- If ending cash > $40,000 → no borrowing; excess can repay loans or cover interest.  
- If ending cash < $30,000 → firm must obtain additional financing.

> [!warning] **Cash‑budget trigger**  
> Do not ignore cash balances between $30k‑$40k; they dictate borrowing vs. repayment decisions.

## Master‑budget process & source hierarchy
| Budget | Primary source |
|--------|----------------|
| Sales | Sales forecast (drives volume) |
| Production (DM, DL, MOH) | Production budget (derived from sales volume) |
| COGS | Unit product cost + ending‑inventory budget |
| SGA | SGA expense budget |
| NOI | Income‑statement before financing |
| Cash | Cash‑budget results (collections, payments, financing) |
| Balance‑sheet items | Directly linked to respective sub‑budgets (AR → collection schedule, inventories → material/finished‑goods budgets, PPE → capital‑expenditure plan) |

> [!info] **Master budget driver**  
> The sales forecast is the single most important input; all other budgets cascade from it.

## Service‑industry budgeting differences
- No direct‑material or production budgets; labor is the main cost driver.  
- Variable costs expressed per service unit (e.g., haircut commission $2.50).  
- Focus on headcount, wages, commissions, and service‑related overhead.

> [!example] **Barber shop scenario**  
> - Employees ↑10%: 9,000 → 9,900.  
> - Unit price $16 → revenue = 9,900 × $16 = $158,400.  
> - Salaries (two tiers) after 5% tax exemption = $35,000 total.  
> - Commission expense = $2.50 × 9,000 = $22,500.  
> - Variable operating‑hour cost = $2,025 (scenario 3.1) or $2,430 (scenario 3.2).

## Balance‑sheet line‑item origins
- **Cash** – from cash‑budget ending balance.  
- **Accounts receivable** – from cash‑collection schedule.  
- **Raw‑material inventory** – from direct‑material budget.  
- **Finished‑goods inventory** – from finished‑goods inventory budget.  
- **PPE** – opening balance + capital‑expenditure additions (e.g., equipment purchases in May/June).

## Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| PLHR | Per‑Labor‑Hour Rate, used to compute direct overhead rate |
| DOHR | Direct Overhead Rate per Direct‑Labor‑Base unit |
| DRB | Direct‑Labor‑Base unit (basis for overhead allocation) |
| COGS | Cost of Goods Sold, derived from unit product cost and inventory budgets |
| NOI | Net Operating Income, profit before financing costs |
| SMA | Sales‑Margin Allowance, a per‑unit price concession |
| PPE | Property, Plant & Equipment, capital assets reflected on the balance sheet |
