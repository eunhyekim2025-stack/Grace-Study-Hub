> [!summary] Key takeaways
- Differential‑cost analysis isolates only items that change between alternatives.  
- Relevant items: incremental costs, differential revenues, avoidable fixed costs; irrelevant: sunk, common, unchanged fixed costs.  
- Follow the 5‑step process: define alternatives → list costs/benefits → classify relevance → compute net relevant profit → choose best.  
- Qualitative factors (time, convenience) can tip the decision when monetary differences are small.  
- The same framework applies to segment‑retention, make‑or‑buy, special‑order, drop‑or‑keep, and resource‑allocation decisions.  

## 1. Differential‑Cost Analysis Overview
- **Goal:** compare alternatives by focusing on costs/benefits that differ.  
- **Approaches:**  
  - *Donor‑forced*: full cost‑benefit view (comprehensive but data‑heavy).  
  - *Differential‑cost*: only relevant items (simpler, decision‑focused).  

## 2. Step‑by‑Step Procedure
> [!info] **Step 1 – Define decision & alternatives**  
- State the decision problem clearly.  
- List every viable alternative (A, B, C …). No analysis if only one option.  

> [!info] **Step 2 – Gather all cost & benefit data**  
- Capture revenues, variable costs, avoidable fixed costs, and any qualitative benefits.  

> [!info] **Step 3 – Classify relevance**  
- **Relevant:** changes with the alternative (incremental costs, differential revenues, avoidable fixed costs, future costs/revenues).  
- **Irrelevant:** sunk costs, common costs, fixed costs unchanged across alternatives.  

> [!info] **Step 4 – Compute net relevant outcome**  
- For each alternative: Relevant revenues – Relevant costs.  
- Avoid double‑counting; compute total profit for each option before comparing.  

> [!info] **Step 5 – Choose the best alternative**  
- Select the option with the highest net relevant benefit (or lowest relevant cost).  

## 3. Relevance Criteria
| Item | Relevant? | Reason |
|------|-----------|--------|
| Incremental (avoidable) cost | Yes | Varies between alternatives |
| Differential revenue | Yes | Earned only under a specific option |
| Future cost/revenue | Yes | Depends on choice |
| Historical/sunk cost | No | Cannot be altered |
| Common cost (e.g., console) | No | Same for all options |
| Fixed cost within relevant range | No | Unchanged unless structural change |
| Fixed cost that changes with decision (e.g., outsourcing) | Yes | Becomes avoidable |

## 4. Common Decision Types & Illustrations
### 4.1 Drive vs. Fly (Travel)
- **Relevant costs:** fuel, airfare, parking, depreciation per mile, time, hassle.  
- **Irrelevant:** original car purchase price, identical insurance.  
- *Result:* Variable cost driving $137 vs. flying $200 → $63 saving driving; qualitative factors may still favor flying.  

### 4.2 Segment Retention (Keep/Drop)
- Identify **unavoidable fixed costs** (remain if dropped) → irrelevant.  
- Variable costs that disappear when dropped → relevant.  
- *Example:* Digital‑watch line – fixed factory overhead and depreciation stay; only variable production costs matter.  

### 4.3 Make‑or‑Buy (Outsourcing)
- **Relevant:** avoidable labor, material, overhead, salary savings.  
- **Irrelevant:** allocated fixed costs, depreciation of equipment.  
- *Numerical example:* In‑house $17/unit vs. outsource $25/unit → $8 saving per unit; 20,000 units → $160 k advantage for in‑house.  

### 4.4 Special Order
- May eliminate sales‑commission → commission becomes irrelevant.  
- Only incremental costs (materials, variable labor) and incremental revenue matter.  

## 5. Qualitative Considerations
- Time, convenience, stress, quality control, proprietary technology, ethical issues.  
- Treat as **benefits** or **costs** in the decision matrix when monetary differences are marginal.  

## 6. Practical Tips
> [!warning] Do not include sunk or common costs in the net calculation; they distort the decision.  
> [!warning] Verify the decision stays within the relevant range; crossing it can turn previously fixed costs into relevant.  
> [!example] **Labor‑saving machine:** Rental $3,000; labor savings $15,000 → net $12,000 benefit → rent the machine.  

## Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Differential cost | Cost that differs between alternatives; incremental/avoidable. |
| Relevant cost | Any cost or benefit that changes with the decision. |
| Irrelevant cost | Sunk, common, or unchanged fixed cost; ignored in analysis. |
| Incremental revenue | Additional revenue earned only under a specific alternative. |
| Relevant range | Activity level where fixed costs remain unchanged. |
