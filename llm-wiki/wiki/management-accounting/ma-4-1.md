> [!summary] Key takeaways  
- Job order costing tracks **direct materials, direct labor, and overhead per unique job**; process costing averages costs over homogeneous units.  
- **Overhead** is allocated via a **cost driver**; choose **actual** or **normal** costing (POHR).  
- **POHR** = Budgeted overhead ÷ Budgeted driver units; apply to each job using actual driver usage.  
- At period‑end, compare **applied** vs. **actual** overhead and adjust **COGS** (or allocate) to eliminate over/under‑applied balances.  
- Fixed overhead is constant for the period; variable overhead varies with activity level.

## Costing Systems Overview

### Job Order Costing
> [!info] **Definition**: Accumulates manufacturing costs for each distinct job; used when products are customized or produced to specific orders.  
- Indicators: distinct specifications, low volume, custom manufacturing.  
- Typical examples: custom aircraft, movie productions, catering with varied menus.  
- **Job‑cost sheet** records:  
  - Direct Materials (DM) – raw inputs, cost = unit × quantity.  
  - Direct Labor (DL) – hours × rate (timesheets).  
  - Manufacturing Overhead (MOH) – indirect costs allocated via a driver.  

### Process Costing
> [!info] **Definition**: Accumulates costs by department/process for homogeneous, high‑volume products.  
- Identical units, continuous flow.  
- Examples: A4 paper, canned soup.  

## Job‑Cost Sheet & Cost Drivers

| Cost Category | What it includes | Typical Driver |
|---------------|------------------|----------------|
| Direct Materials | Raw inputs traceable to job | Quantity or cost of material |
| Direct Labor | Labor hours on job | Labor hours |
| Manufacturing Overhead | Indirect utilities, depreciation, rent, etc. | DLH, machine hours, material cost |

> [!example] **Normal costing** – POHR calculation  
Budgeted overhead = $40,000; budgeted DLH = 10,000 → POHR = **$4 per DLH**.  
Job uses 8 DLH → Applied overhead = 8 × $4 = **$32**.

> [!example] **Total job cost**  
DM $100 + DL $15 + Overhead $32 = **$147**.  
If 2 units produced → Average cost = $147 ÷ 2 = **$73.5** per unit.

## Overhead Application (Actual vs. Normal)

> [!info] **Actual costing** – Apply the *actual* overhead incurred to a job using the *actual* amount of the chosen driver.  
> [!info] **Normal costing** – Use a **predetermined overhead rate (POHR)** calculated before the period; multiply POHR by the job’s actual driver usage.

### Steps for Normal Costing
1. Compute POHR = Budgeted overhead ÷ Budgeted driver units.  
2. Record actual driver usage per job.  
3. Applied Overhead = POHR × Actual driver usage.  
4. Total Job Cost = DM + DL + Applied Overhead.  

> [!warning] **Pitfall**: Using the POHR assumes the budgeted driver level is realistic; large variances cause over‑ or under‑applied overhead.

## Period‑End Overhead Reconciliation

1. **Determine applied overhead** for the period (sum of all jobs’ applied MOH).  
2. **Determine actual overhead** incurred (sum of all overhead expenses).  
3. **Compare**:  
   - Applied > Actual → **Over‑applied** (product cost overstated).  
   - Applied < Actual → **Under‑applied** (product cost understated).  

### Closing the Difference
| Method | Where the adjustment goes |
|--------|---------------------------|
| Close to COGM | Adjust Cost of Goods Manufactured account |
| Close to COGS (common) | Adjust Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) directly |
| Proportionate allocation | Distribute adjustment among WIP, Finished Goods, and COGS based on ending balances |

> [!example] **Over‑applied overhead**  
Applied = $150,000; Actual = $120,000 → Over‑applied $30,000.  
Adjusted COGS = Unadjusted COGS $130,000 – $30,000 = **$100,000**.

> [!example] **Under‑applied overhead**  
Applied = $1,160,000; Actual = $1,210,000 → Under‑applied $50,000.  
Adjusted COGS = Unadjusted COGS + $50,000.

## Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Job Order Costing | Costing method that accumulates costs per individual, custom job |
| Process Costing | Costing method that averages costs over homogeneous units produced in a continuous flow |
| Direct Materials | Raw inputs traceable directly to a job |
| Direct Labor | Labor hours and rates directly tied to a job |
| Manufacturing Overhead (MOH) | Indirect costs allocated to jobs via a cost driver |
| Predetermined Overhead Rate (POHR) | Budgeted overhead ÷ budgeted driver units, used in normal costing |
| Over‑applied Overhead | Applied overhead exceeds actual; COGS must be reduced |
| Under‑applied Overhead | Applied overhead is less than actual; COGS must be increased |
