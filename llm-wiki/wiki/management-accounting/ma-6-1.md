## 2. Cost‑of‑Goods schedules  
> [!info] **COGM schedule** – computes total cost of goods manufactured (all production costs).  
> [!info] **COGS schedule** – computes cost of goods sold (finished‑goods balance).  
- Both belong to the overall cost‑allocation plan.

## 3. Service‑department cost allocation  

### Methods overview  

| Method | Allocation direction | Inter‑service recognition |
|--------|---------------------|---------------------------|
| **Direct** | Service → Operating only | None |
| **Step‑down** | Pre‑determined order (A → B → C) | Partial; once a dept’s cost is stepped down, it never receives a later allocation |
| **Reciprocal** | Simultaneous equations | Full (all inter‑service interactions) |

> [!warning] **Step‑down rule** – never allocate back to a service department that has already distributed its cost.  

> [!info] **Step‑down calculation** – allocate **original cost + any costs received earlier**. After the last step, each service department’s balance is zero and total cost is unchanged.

### Example (illustrative)  
> [!example]  
- HR cost RM 27 k → Water dept.  
- Admin cost RM 41 k → Water dept.  
- Water dept. original cost RM 550 k.  
- Post‑allocation Water cost = 550 k + 27 k + 41 k = **RM 618 k**.  

## 4. Costing systems  

| System | When to use | Cost accumulation |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Job‑order** | Customized, unique products | Per individual job |
| **Process** | Homogeneous products (chemicals, beverages) | By processing department or whole process |

> [!info] **Processing department** – a distinct segment where at least one of DM, DL, MOH is added; output is homogeneous and transferred onward.  

### Orange‑processing illustration  
1. **Cleaning** – adds DM (oranges) ± labor.  
2. **Juicing** – adds labor/overhead.  
3. **Packaging** – adds bottles, caps, labor.  
After each stage, the partially completed batch moves to the next department with its accumulated cost.

## 5. Weighted‑average process costing  

### Core concepts  
> [!info] **Equivalent units (EU)** – Units × % completion.  
> [!info] **Weighted‑average method** – blends beginning WIP costs with period costs; does not separate them.  

### Calculation steps (standard flow)  
1. **Determine EU** for each cost category (material, conversion).  
2. **Add** beginning‑period costs to costs incurred during the period.  
3. **Cost per EU** = Total cost ÷ Total EU (separate for material & conversion).  
4. **Allocate** costs to units completed & transferred out and to ending WIP.  

> [!example]  
- Beginning inventory: 300 units (40 % material, 20 % conversion).  
- Costs: Material $6,000; Conversion $4,000.  
- Period activity: Started 6,000 units; Completed & transferred 5,400; Ending WIP 900 (2 % material, 30 % conversion).  
- Added costs: Material $118,000; Conversion $81,000.  

**EU calculations**  
- Completed: 5,400 EU (both categories).  
- Ending WIP: Material 18 EU (900 × 2 %); Conversion 270 EU (900 × 30 %).  
- Total EU – Material = 5,418; Conversion = 5,670.  

**Cost per EU**  
- Material: ($6,000 + $118,000) ÷ 5,418 ≈ $22.9 (lecture rounded to $1.00 for illustration).  
- Conversion: ($4,000 + $81,000) ÷ 5,670 ≈ $15.0.  

**Unit cost** = Material + Conversion ≈ $36 (lecture used $35).  

### Unit‑flow relationship (quick check)  
\[
\text{Beginning WIP} + \text{Units Started} - \text{Ending WIP} = \text{Units Completed (Transferred)}
\]

## 6. Material‑cost‑per‑EU quick problem  

> [!example]  
- Beginning material cost $500; added material $3,000.  
- Units completed = 800; ending WIP = 200 (70 % material).  
- EU = 800 + 0.7 × 200 = 1,000.  
- Material cost per EU = ($500 + $3,000) ÷ 1,000 = **$3** (answer B).  

## Key terms  

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| COGM | Cost of Goods Manufactured – total production cost for the period |
| COGS | Cost of Goods Sold – cost of inventory that was sold |
| Direct method | Service‑dept allocation straight to operating depts, ignoring inter‑service links |
| Step‑down method | Sequential allocation; each dept’s cost includes prior allocations, no back‑allocation |
| Reciprocal method | Full inter‑service allocation solved via simultaneous equations |
| Job‑order costing | Cost accumulation per individual job (custom products) |
| Process costing | Cost accumulation by department for homogeneous products |
| Equivalent unit | Measure of work done expressed as fully completed units |
| Weighted‑average method | Blends beginning WIP and period costs to compute cost per EU |
