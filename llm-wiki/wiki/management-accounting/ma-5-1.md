> [!summary] Key takeaways  
- CVP ties cost, volume, price, and contribution margin; master it to solve any cost‑volume problem.  
- Absorption costing capitalizes all manufacturing overhead; variable costing expenses fixed overhead immediately.  
- Inventory differences create a predictable NOI gap: +fixed OH × (Prod – Sales) under absorption.  
- Normal costing uses a budgeted overhead rate; over/under‑applied overhead is recorded as a variance.  
- Variable‑costing statements (contribution‑margin format) are preferred for internal decision‑making.

## Cost‑behavior & CVP basics
> [!info] **Cost behavior** – the way costs change with activity level; essential for CVP analysis.  
- CVP equation: **Profit = (Sales – Variable Costs) – Fixed Costs**.  
- Contribution margin = Sales – Total variable expenses; drives break‑even and target‑profit calculations.  

## Overhead costing
> [!info] **Overhead rate** = Total overhead cost ÷ Total actual activity.  
- Apply overhead: **Overhead Rate × Activity consumed** by a job.  
- Allocation bases:  
  - **Departmental** – department‑specific rate × department activity.  
  - **Behavioral** – fixed‑MOH rate × activity (same calculation, different base).  

## Absorption vs. Variable (direct) costing
> [!info] **Absorption costing** – all manufacturing overhead (fixed + variable) is treated as product cost.  
> [!info] **Variable costing** – only variable manufacturing overhead is product cost; fixed overhead is a period expense.  

- **Product‑cost composition**  

| Costing method | Direct materials | Direct labor | Variable MOH | Fixed MOH |
|----------------|------------------|--------------|--------------|----------|
| Absorption     | ✔︎                | ✔︎          | ✔︎            | ✔︎        |
| Variable       | ✔︎                | ✔︎          | ✔︎            | ✘ (period) |

- **Inventory effect** – Absorption includes fixed MOH in inventory, raising balance‑sheet values; variable excludes it.  

## Income‑statement formats
> [!info] **Absorption (full) costing statement** – shows COGS with all product costs, then gross profit, then selling & admin expenses.  
> [!info] **Variable (contribution) costing statement** – shows sales, total variable expenses, contribution margin, then fixed expenses.  

### Example: Harvey Company (25 000 units produced)

| Item | Variable costing | Absorption costing |
|------|------------------|--------------------|
| Unit product cost | $10 (DM + DL + Var MOH) | $16 ($10 + $6 fixed) |
| Units produced | 25 000 | 25 000 |
| Units sold | 20 000 | 20 000 |
| COGS | $200 000 | $320 000 |
| Sales (20 000 × $30) | $600 000 | $600 000 |
| Variable S&A | $60 000 | $60 000 |
| Fixed S&A | $100 000 | $100 000 |
| Fixed MOH | $150 000 | $150 000 |
| **Net operating income** | **$90 000** | **$120 000** |

- Difference $30 000 = fixed MOH per unit ($6) × ending inventory (5 000 units).  

## Reconciliation of NOI
> [!info] **Reconciliation formula**  
`Absorption NOI = Variable NOI ± (Fixed OH / unit × (Units produced – Units sold))`  

- **Inventory increase** (Prod > Sales) → add fixed OH × ΔInv → absorption NOI higher.  
- **Inventory decrease** (Prod < Sales) → subtract → absorption NOI lower.  

### Year‑2 scenario (sell 30 000, produce 25 000)
- Fixed OH per unit = $6; ΔInv = –5 000 → subtract $30 000.  
- Absorption NOI = Variable NOI – $30 000 → $138 000 (absorption) vs. $168 000 (variable).  

## Normal costing
> [!info] **Normal costing** – uses a predetermined overhead rate based on budgeted activity, applied to actual activity.  
- **Predetermined rate** = Budgeted overhead ÷ Budgeted activity.  
- **Applied overhead** = Predetermined rate × Actual activity.  
- **Variance** = Applied overhead – Actual overhead; recorded as over‑ or under‑applied overhead at period end.  

> [!warning] **Profit manipulation risk** – Raising production spreads fixed overhead over more units, lowering unit cost under absorption and inflating profit; variable costing eliminates this distortion.  

## Practical implications
- External financial reporting **requires absorption costing**.  
- Internal management analysis prefers **variable costing** for clear CVP insight and to avoid profit manipulation.  
- Firms must weigh the **cost of preparing multiple reports** against the decision‑making benefit.  

## Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| CVP | Cost‑Volume‑Profit analysis linking cost, volume, price, and contribution margin |
| Overhead rate | Total overhead ÷ Total activity (actual or budgeted) |
| Absorption costing | All manufacturing overhead (fixed + variable) treated as product cost |
| Variable costing | Only variable manufacturing overhead is product cost; fixed overhead is period expense |
| Contribution margin | Sales – Total variable expenses |
| Normal costing | Uses a predetermined overhead rate; variance adjusted at period end |
| Over/under‑applied overhead | Difference between overhead applied using the predetermined rate and actual overhead incurred |
