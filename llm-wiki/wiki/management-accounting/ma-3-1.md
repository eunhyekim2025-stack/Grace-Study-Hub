> [!summary] Key takeaways  
- Compute break‑even first; then use the same logic for target‑profit analysis.  
- Required sales = (Target profit + Fixed) ÷ Contribution‑margin ratio (or per‑unit CM).  
- Margin of safety = Actual sales − Break‑even sales; % = 1 − (BE / Actual).  
- DOL = Contribution margin ÷ Net operating income; high DOL ⇔ high fixed‑cost risk.  
- For multiple products, solve the whole‑company break‑even then allocate using the constant sales‑mix (dollar or unit).

## Prerequisite knowledge
> [!info] Contribution‑income statement components  
- Sales, variable expenses, contribution margin, fixed expenses, net operating income.  
- Variable costs and sales move proportionally with volume; contribution margin is also volume‑proportional.

## Target‑Profit Analysis
> [!info] Goal: find required unit sales and required dollar sales to hit a profit target.  

**Formulas**  
| Variable | Formula |
|---|---|
| Required units (Q) | (Target profit + Fixed expenses) ÷ Contribution‑margin per unit |
| Required sales ($) | (Target profit + Fixed expenses) ÷ Contribution‑margin ratio |

> [!example] Bike company  
- Current profit = $20,000; Desired profit = $40,000.  
- Fixed expenses = $80,000 → Target CM = $40,000 + $80,000 = $120,000.  
- CM ratio = 40 % → Required sales = $120,000 ÷ 0.40 = **$300,000**.  
- CM per unit = $200 → Required units = $120,000 ÷ $200 = **600 units** (note: instructor’s numeric example gave 400 units; adjust to your data).

> [!warning] Do **not** memorize formulas only; understand the logical steps (add fixed costs to profit target, then divide by the appropriate margin).

### Target‑profit percentage
- Expresses required sales as a % of current sales.  
- Example: 120 % target‑profit % → need 120 % of current sales ($250,000) to reach $40,000 profit.  
- Computation: (Target CM ÷ Current CM) × 100 % = Required sales ÷ Current sales.

## Break‑Even Analysis
> [!info] Break‑even point (BEP) = sales where profit = $0.  

**Formulas** (same as target‑profit with profit = 0)  
| Variable | Formula |
|---|---|
| BE units | Fixed expenses ÷ Contribution‑margin per unit |
| BE sales ($) | Fixed expenses ÷ Contribution‑margin ratio |

> [!example] Fixed expenses $80,000, CM per unit $200, CM ratio 40 % → BE = 400 units or $200,000 sales.

### Break‑even percentage
- Proportion of current sales needed to cover fixed costs.  
- Calculation: (Fixed expenses ÷ Current contribution margin) × 100 % → 80 % in the example.

## Margin of Safety
> [!info] Buffer between actual (or projected) sales and break‑even sales.  

**Formulas**  
- Margin of safety (units) = Actual units − BE units.  
- Margin of safety ($) = Actual sales − BE sales.  
- Margin‑of‑safety % = (Actual − BE) ÷ Actual = 1 − (BE ÷ Actual).

> [!example] BE sales $1,500; Actual sales $3,000 → MOS = $1,500 (50 %).

## Degree of Operating Leverage (DOL)
> [!info] Measures how a % change in sales translates to a % change in net operating income (NOI).  

**Formula**  
\[
\text{DOL} = \frac{\%\Delta \text{NOI}}{\%\Delta \text{Sales}} = \frac{\text{Contribution margin}}{\text{Net operating income}}
\]

> [!example] CM = $100 k, NOI = $20 k → DOL = 5.  
- Interpretation: 1 % sales rise → 5 % NOI rise; 10 % sales rise → 50 % NOI rise.

### Cost‑structure impact
| Cost structure | DOL | Risk profile |
|---|---|---|
| High fixed, low variable | High | Large profit swings (high risk) |
| Low fixed, high variable | Low | Smoother earnings (low risk) |

> [!warning] DOL is **sales‑level specific**; it changes if the sales base changes.

## Multi‑Product Break‑Even Analysis
> [!info] Assumes sales‑mix percentages stay constant unless stated otherwise.  

### Sales‑mix
- **Dollar mix**: proportion of total sales dollars per product (e.g., Bicycle 45 %).  
- **Unit mix**: proportion of total units sold per product (e.g., 50 % of units).  
- Use the mix that matches the base you are allocating (dollar vs. unit).

### Two solution methods
**Method 1 – Break‑Even % Method**  
1. BE % = Fixed expenses ÷ Total contribution margin.  
2. BE sales = BE % × Total sales.  
3. Allocate BE sales to each product using the **dollar** sales‑mix.

**Method 2 – Contribution‑Margin Ratio Method**  
1. CMR = Total contribution margin ÷ Total sales.  
2. BE sales = Fixed expenses ÷ CMR.  
3. Allocate BE sales using the **dollar** sales‑mix (same result as Method 1).

> [!example] Fixed = $170 k, Total CM = $265 k, Total sales = $550 k.  
- BE % = 170 k ÷ 265 k ≈ 64.15 %.  
- BE sales = 0.6415 × 550 k ≈ $352 k.  
- With 45 % bicycle mix → Bicycle BE sales ≈ $158 k; Car BE sales ≈ $194 k.

> [!warning] Do **not** apply the unit‑mix percentage to dollar‑sales allocation (or vice‑versa).

## Summary of analytical sequence
1. Compute **break‑even** (units & dollars).  
2. Determine **target‑profit** requirements using the same structure.  
3. Calculate **margin of safety** to assess risk buffer.  
4. Evaluate **DOL** to gauge earnings sensitivity.  
5. For multiple products, solve the **company‑wide break‑even** then distribute via the appropriate **sales‑mix**.

## Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Contribution margin (CM) | Sales − Variable expenses; per unit or total |
| Contribution‑margin ratio (CMR) | CM ÷ Sales (percentage) |
| Break‑Even Point (BEP) | Sales level where profit = $0 |
| Margin of Safety (MOS) | Actual sales − BEP; expressed in $/units or % |
| Degree of Operating Leverage (DOL) | CM ÷ Net operating income; measures profit sensitivity |
| Sales‑mix (dollar vs. unit) | Fixed proportion of each product’s sales in dollars or units |
