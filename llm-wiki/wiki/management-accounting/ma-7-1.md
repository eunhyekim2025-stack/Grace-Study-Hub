> [!summary] Key takeaways  
- ABC uses multiple activity cost pools and drivers, giving more accurate overhead than a single plant‑wide rate.  
- Costs are allocated **only** when a product or customer actually consumes the activity.  
- Five activity levels (unit, batch, product, customer, organization) guide pool creation.  
- The 5‑step ABC process: define activities → pool allocation → rate calculation → cost‑object assignment → margin reporting.  
- Example: Baxter’s “SureStart” and “Long Life” batteries show how ABC reveals the true profit‑driving product.

## 1. ABC vs. Traditional Costing
- Traditional: one overhead pool, allocated by a volume base (units, labor h, machine h).  
- ABC: several pools, each tied to a specific activity driver → better cost‑object matching.  

> [!info] **Cost object** – the item (product, customer, order) whose cost we want to determine.

## 2. Activity Levels & Drivers
| Level | Typical costs | Typical driver |
|------|---------------|----------------|
| Unit‑level | Direct material, direct labor | Units produced |
| Batch‑level | Setup, cleaning | Number of batches |
| Product‑level | Design, R&D | Number of design changes |
| Customer‑level | Dedicated sales visits | Active customers |
| Organization‑level | Corporate overhead | *none* (not allocated) |

> [!info] **Transaction driver** – simple count (e.g., orders).  
> [!info] **Duration driver** – time‑based measure (e.g., machine hours).

## 3. ABC Implementation Steps

### Step 1 – Define activities & drivers
- Identify every activity that consumes resources (e.g., order processing, design changes, unit production, customer relations).  
- Choose a driver that best reflects consumption (orders → #orders, design → #changes, unit → machine h, etc.).

> [!example] *Baxter activity list* – order processing (batch), design changes (product), unit production (unit), customer relations (customer), corporate support (organization).

### Step 2 – Allocate indirect costs to activity pools
- Exclude direct material, labor, and shipping.  
- Assign costs **directly traceable** to an activity fully to that pool.  
- For mixed‑use resources, estimate usage percentages (cross‑functional interviews) and allocate proportionally.  

> [!warning] Do **not** allocate any cost to a pool if the activity never uses that resource.

### Step 3 – Compute activity rates
\[
\text{Activity rate} = \frac{\text{Cost pool}}{\text{Total driver quantity}}
\]

| Activity | Cost pool | Driver total | Rate |
|----------|-----------|--------------|------|
| Customer orders | $4.52 M | 10,000 orders | $452 per order |
| Design changes | $3.00 M | 4,000 changes | $760 per change |
| Machine hours | $5.20 M | 800,000 h | $6.50 per hour |
| Customer relations | $3.08 M | 2,000 customers | $1,540 per customer |
| Org‑level | $X | – | – (no allocation) |

### Step 4 – Allocate to cost objects
- Multiply each object’s driver usage by the corresponding rate.  
- Sum across all activities to obtain total overhead for the object.  

> [!example] *SureStart battery*  
- Orders: 4,000 × $452 = $1.81 M  
- Design changes: 0 × $760 = $0  
- Machine hours: 480,000 × $6.50 = $3.12 M  
- Total ABC overhead ≈ $4.9 M (plus direct material & labor).

> [!example] *Long Life battery* receives the remaining overhead (≈ $7–8 M).

### Step 5 – Prepare management report
- Add direct material & labor to the allocated overhead → total product cost.  
- Subtract from product revenue → product margin.  
- Combine product margins to verify overall company profit/loss.  

> [!info] **Key principle** – greater activity usage → higher allocated cost; zero usage → zero allocation.

## 4. Customer‑Level Profitability (Second‑stage allocation)
1. Identify the customer as the cost object.  
2. Use the same driver‑rate multiplication (e.g., #active customers × $1,540).  
3. Add direct costs and revenue to compute each customer’s margin.  
4. Segment customers (retail vs. corporate) to spot loss‑making groups.  

> [!warning] Profitability analysis should also weigh strategic factors (growth potential, market importance) beyond pure margin.

## 5. Quick Reference – ABC Formulae
- **Activity rate** = Cost pool ÷ Driver quantity  
- **Cost allocated** = Activity rate × Object’s driver usage  
- **Total product cost** = Direct material + Direct labor + Σ allocated activity costs  

## Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Cost object | Item (product, customer, order) whose cost is being measured |
| Activity pool | Group of costs tied to a single activity |
| Activity driver | Metric that reflects consumption of an activity |
| Transaction driver | Count‑based driver (e.g., #orders) |
| Duration driver | Time‑based driver (e.g., machine hours) |
| Unit‑level activity | Costs incurred each time a unit is produced |
| Batch‑level activity | Costs incurred each time a batch is processed |
| Product‑level activity | Costs incurred for a product regardless of volume |
| Customer‑level activity | Costs incurred to serve a specific customer |
| Organization‑level activity | Corporate overhead not allocated to cost objects |
