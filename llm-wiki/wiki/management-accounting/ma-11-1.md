> [!summary] Key takeaways  
- Static budget is fixed; flexible budget adapts to actual activity.  
- Activity variance compares static vs flexible; revenue & spending variances compare actual vs flexible.  
- Price variance uses actual quantity; quantity (efficiency) variance uses standard price.  
- Favorable = F (better than benchmark); Unfavorable = U (worse).  
- Assign responsibility based on variance source (production vs purchasing).

## 1. Variance‑Analysis Cycle
- **Plan**: prepare a **static (master) budget** before the period; remains unchanged.  
- **Execute**: record actual output, revenues, and costs.  
- **Flex**: build a **flexible budget** by inserting the actual activity level into cost/revenue formulas.  
- **Analyze**: compute activity, revenue, and spending variances; then decompose cost variances into price and quantity components.  

> [!info] **Static (master) budget** – prepared in advance; cannot explain cost changes caused by different activity levels.  

> [!warning] A static budget alone cannot isolate the effect of volume changes; always follow with a flexible budget for meaningful analysis.

## 2. Static vs Flexible Budget
| Budget type | When built | Benchmark for variance |
|------------|------------|------------------------|
| Static (master) | Before period | – |
| Flexible | After actual activity known | Used for revenue & spending variances |
| Activity variance | Static vs Flexible | **Static budget** |

- **Flexible‑budget formula**:  
  - Revenue = *price* × *actual units*  
  - Variable cost = *variable‑cost rate* × *actual units*  
  - Fixed cost = unchanged (within relevant range)  

> [!example] **Larry’s Lawn Services** – static budget assumed 500 services → $500 expense. Actual = 550 services; flexible budget recomputes each line (e.g., revenue = $75 × 550). The $50 difference is the activity variance.

## 3. Types of Variances
| Variance | Benchmark | Interpretation |
|----------|-----------|----------------|
| **Activity (volume) variance** | Static budget | F if flexible > static (more activity); U if less. |
| **Revenue (price) variance** | Flexible budget | F if actual revenue > flexible; U otherwise. |
| **Spending (cost) variance** | Flexible budget | F if actual cost < flexible; U otherwise. |
| **Price variance** | Standard price | (Actual price – Standard price) × Actual quantity |
| **Quantity (efficiency) variance** | Standard quantity | (Actual quantity – Standard quantity) × Standard price |

> [!info] **F/U labeling** – use “F” for favorable, “U” for unfavorable; do **not** display minus signs.

## 4. Standard Cost & Price/Quantity Variances
- **Standard cost** = predetermined *price* (rate) + *quantity* (usage) for each input.  
- Set per period using history, engineering studies, or best‑practice estimates.  

**Formulas**

| Variance | Formula |
|----------|---------|
| Price variance | (Actual price – Standard price) × Actual quantity |
| Quantity (efficiency) variance | (Actual quantity – Standard quantity) × Standard price |

- Use **actual quantity** for price variance (purchasing control).  
- Use **standard price** for quantity variance (production control).  

> [!warning] Fixed‑cost variance is separate; fixed costs do not change with activity, so they are not part of price/quantity analysis.

## 5. Interpretation & Responsibility
- **Favorable revenue variance** → higher sales price or volume than expected → credit to sales/marketing.  
- **Unfavorable spending variance** → higher actual cost than flexible budget → investigate price vs. quantity causes.  
- **Price variance** → responsibility of purchasing (price control).  
- **Quantity (efficiency) variance** → responsibility of production/operations (usage control).  
- **Activity variance** → overall planning accuracy; may trigger strategic review.

> [!info] **Root‑cause isolation** – first separate activity effect, then split cost variances into price and quantity to pinpoint who can act.

## Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Static (master) budget | Fixed budget prepared before the period; unchanged regardless of actual activity |
| Flexible budget | Budget recalculated at actual activity level; enables meaningful variance analysis |
| Activity (volume) variance | Difference between static and flexible budgets; measures impact of volume change |
| Price variance | (Actual price – Standard price) × Actual quantity; reflects purchasing performance |
| Quantity (efficiency) variance | (Actual quantity – Standard quantity) × Standard price; reflects production efficiency |
| Favorable (F) / Unfavorable (U) | Labels indicating variance direction relative to benchmark |
