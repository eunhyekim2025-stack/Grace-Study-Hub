> [!summary] Key takeaways  
- Variance analysis uses AQ, SQ, AP, SP to isolate price and quantity effects.  
- Favorable = actual cost lower (or revenue higher) than standard; Unfavorable = opposite.  
- Material & labor variances follow the same two‑formula pattern; overhead adds spending & volume components.  
- Management by exception focuses on variances that breach preset tolerance limits.  
- Follow the four‑step process: gather data → compute → label → aggregate.

## Variance Analysis Basics
- **Inputs**: Actual Quantity (AQ), Standard Quantity (SQ), Actual Price (AP), Standard Price (SP).  
- **Formulas**  
  - Price variance = (AP − SP) × AQ  
  - Quantity variance = (AQ − SQ) × SP  
- **Favorable (F)**: reduces total cost or raises revenue.  
- **Unfavorable (U)**: increases total cost or lowers revenue.  

> [!info] **Price Variance** = (Actual Price – Standard Price) × Actual Quantity.  

> [!info] **Quantity Variance** = (Actual Quantity – Standard Quantity) × Standard Price.  

> [!warning] Do **not** mix purchase quantity with usage quantity: price variance uses purchase amount; quantity variance uses usage amount.

## Material Example
> [!example] **Manufacturing firm – 2,000 units**  

| Item | Standard | Actual |
|------|----------|--------|
| SQ (kg) | 0.1 kg × 2,000 = 200 kg | AQ = 210 kg |
| SP ($/kg) | $5.00 | AP = $4.90 |

- **Price variance** = (4.90 − 5.00) × 210 = **‑$21 F**  
- **Quantity variance** = (210 − 200) × 5 = **+$50 U**  
- **Net variance** = $29 U  

*Alternative check*:  
Actual cost = 210 kg × $4.90 = $1,029  
Standard cost for AQ = 210 kg × $5 = $1,050 → $21 F (same result).

## Labor Variance
- Same two‑formula structure, using rates and hours.  

> [!info] **Labor‑rate variance** = (Actual rate – Standard rate) × Actual hours.  

> [!info] **Labor‑efficiency variance** = (Actual hours – Standard hours) × Standard rate.  

> [!example] **Standard**: RM 10/hr, 2,400 hr; **Actual**: RM 10.50/hr, 2,500 hr  
- Rate variance = (10.50 − 10) × 2,500 = **RM 1,250 U**  
- Efficiency variance = (2,500 − 2,400) × 10 = **RM 1,000 F**

## Overhead Variance
- Distinguish **spending** (price) from **volume** (efficiency).  

> [!info] **POHR** = Budgeted Fixed Overhead ÷ Budgeted Allocation Base.  

> [!info] **Applied overhead** = POHR × Actual activity.  

> [!info] **Volume variance** = (Actual activity – Budgeted activity) × POHR.  

> [!example] **Budgeted fixed overhead** = $150 K; **Actual** = $160 K → **$10 K U** spending variance.  

> [!example] POHR = $150 K ÷ 10 K units = $15/unit.  
Actual activity = 12,000 units → Applied overhead = $180 K.  
Volume variance = (12,000 − 10,000) × $15 = **$30 K F** (better capacity use).

## Management by Exception
- Companies set **tolerance limits** (percentage or dollar) for each cost element.  
- Only variances beyond these limits trigger investigation.  
- Example: if material is 65 % of product cost, a 1 % deviation may be material and thus examined.  

> [!info] **Budget variance** (fixed‑budget) = Actual fixed cost – Budgeted fixed cost.  

> [!info] **Volume (activity) variance** = (Actual activity – Budgeted activity) × Standard cost per unit.

## Practical Steps for Students
1. Identify AQ, SQ, AP, SP from the problem.  
2. Apply price and quantity formulas.  
3. Label each result **F** or **U** based on cost direction.  
4. Add price and quantity variances for the total cost element variance.  
5. For overhead, compute spending and volume variances using POHR.  

## Key Terms
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| AQ | Actual Quantity used or purchased |
| SQ | Standard Quantity allowed per unit of activity |
| AP | Actual Price paid (or actual rate) |
| SP | Standard (budgeted) Price or rate |
| POHR | Predetermined Overhead Rate (budgeted fixed overhead ÷ budgeted allocation base) |
| F / U | Favorable (cost ↓ or revenue ↑) / Unfavorable (cost ↑ or revenue ↓) |
| Tolerance limit | Pre‑set threshold that triggers variance investigation |
