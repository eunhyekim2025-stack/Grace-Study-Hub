> [!summary] Key takeaways  
- Depreciation can be front‑loaded; total tax over an asset’s life stays constant.  
- Capacity = 60 min / (weighted longest step); product mix changes the bottleneck.  
- Line balancing distributes work to meet target cycle time; repeat whenever demand shifts.  
- Build a flexible labor pool and phase‑adjust cycle times to smooth demand spikes.  

## 1. Cost & depreciation considerations  

> [!info] **Economic life vs fiscal (tax) life**  
- Economic life: period the asset actually generates value.  
- Fiscal life: tax‑depreciation schedule, often longer.  

> [!info] **Front‑loading depreciation**  
- Allocate larger depreciation early → lower early‑year taxable income.  
- Cumulative tax over the asset’s life unchanged.  

> [!warning] **Fixed‑cost assumption**  
- Fixed and variable costs may vary over time; treat them as time‑dependent in forecasts.  

> [!example] Depreciation schedule for a $150 k machine  
| Year | Depreciation |
|------|--------------|
| 1    | $70 k |
| 2    | $30 k |
| 3‑5  | $20 k each |

> [!info] **Inflation impact**  
- $1 today ≈ $1.5 in one year; adjust all cash flows to a common price level before comparison.  

## 2. Mixed‑product capacity analysis  

> [!info] **Effective process probability** – weighted average of step times based on product mix.  

**Example mix X = 80 %, Y = 20 %**  

| Step | X time (min) | Y time (min) | Weighted time |
|------|--------------|--------------|---------------|
| 1    | 2            | 3            | 0.8·2 + 0.2·3 = 2.2 |
| 2    | 3            | 2            | 0.8·3 + 0.2·2 = 2.8 |
| 3    | 1            | 1            | 1.0 |

- **Bottleneck** = step with longest weighted time (Step 2).  
- **Capacity** = 60 min / 2.8 min ≈ **21 units/h**.  

> [!warning] **Ignoring mix** – using single‑product times can misidentify the bottleneck.  

**Mix shift example** X = 40 %, Y = 60 % → weighted times change, bottleneck may move to Step 1, raising line capacity from ~20 units/h to ~23 units/h.  

## 3. Bottleneck identification & line balancing  

> [!info] **Line balancing** – allocate tasks so each workstation’s workload ≈ takt time, minimizing idle time.  

- Identify bottleneck station → target improvements there first.  
- Options: add worker, redistribute tasks, automate.  
- Respect logical sequence; do not swap tasks that break precedence.  

> [!example] Target cycle time 40 s, current station times 27 s, 46 s, 37 s → bottleneck is 46 s station. Add a worker or shift work to meet 40 s demand.  

> [!warning] **Single‑add‑worker limit** – adding a second worker to the same station often infeasible; prefer task re‑allocation.  

**Balancing steps (repeat each demand change)**  
1. Confirm updated demand forecast.  
2. Set new production target (units/h).  
3. Re‑balance tasks to meet target cycle time.  

Automation reduces variability, making balancing more stable.  

## 4. Demand planning & capacity flexibility  

- **Level demand**: smooth production to avoid peaks/valleys.  
- **Phase planning**: e.g., first 3 months @ 60 u/h, next 3 months @ 30 u/h → adjust cycle times accordingly.  

> [!info] **Capacity flexibility** – maintain a pool of cross‑trained supervisors/workers ready for rapid deployment.  

- Hire/train staff ahead of expected spikes (e.g., 3‑month horizon).  
- Use demand‑learning/forecasting to stabilise demand levels.  

> [!warning] **Volatile demand** – without flexible capacity, quality and on‑time delivery suffer.  

## Key terms  

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Economic life | Actual useful period of an asset for production. |
| Fiscal (tax) life | Period over which tax depreciation is allowed. |
| Front‑loading depreciation | Allocating larger depreciation expense early in an asset’s life. |
| Effective process probability | Weighted average step time based on product mix. |
| Bottleneck | Station with the longest effective processing time; limits overall capacity. |
| Line balancing | Distributing work to match takt time and minimise idle time. |
| Capacity flexibility | Ability to quickly adjust labor/machine resources to demand changes. |
