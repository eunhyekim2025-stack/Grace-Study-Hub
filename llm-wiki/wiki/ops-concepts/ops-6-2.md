> [!summary] Key takeaways  
- Use a method (stationary, seasonal, or trend) that matches the demand pattern; never rely on last year’s raw demand.  
- Seasonal factors = season‑average ÷ overall average; apply growth multiplier for forward forecasts.  
- Linear regression line y = A + Bx minimizes squared deviations; A = intercept, B = slope.  
- Report intermediate calculations to 4 dp, final answer to 2 dp; method correctness outweighs minor numeric errors.  
- “Istikamah” means consistency – a reminder to apply the chosen forecasting method uniformly.

## 1. Forecasting families

- **Stationary (moving‑average)** – best when demand shows no clear trend or seasonality.  
- **Seasonal** – replace stationary when recurring patterns exist (any length, not just four quarters).  
- **Trend** – apply linear regression when demand rises/falls steadily over time.

> [!warning] Using last year’s demand directly repeats past errors; it fails when patterns shift.

## 2. Seasonal forecasting

- **Seasonality definition** – any predictable, recurring pattern; number and length of seasons are flexible.  
- **Seasonal factor calculation**  
  - Overall average demand = 1390 units.  
  - Quarter 1 average = 1247 units.  
  - Factor = 1247 / 1390 ≈ 0.90 (winter low‑demand).  
- **Growth adjustment** – e.g., 18 % growth → 1390 × 1.18 ≈ 1408 units.  
- **Forecast example** – Q2 2018: 1.048 × 1.408 ≈ 1.48 (seasonal multiplier × base forecast).  

> [!example] Incorporating 28 months of weekly sales refines seasonal factors, improving accuracy.

## 3. Trend forecasting (linear regression)

- **Model**: y = A + Bx  
  - *A* = intercept (forecast when x = 0).  
  - *B* = slope (change per period).  
- **Least‑squares principle** – choose A and B that minimize Σ(observed − predicted)².  
- **Data requirements** – X (time periods) must be consecutive; gaps break the calculation.  

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| y | Historical demand (dependent variable) |
| x | Time period (independent variable) |
| A | Intercept |
| B | Slope |

> [!info] Excel’s `LINEST` or Data Analysis tool returns A and B automatically; the exact formula isn’t needed for the exam.
