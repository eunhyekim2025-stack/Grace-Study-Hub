> [!summary] Key takeaways  
- Little’s Law converts between arrival rate, average time, and average number in any system part.  
- Utilization ρ = λ / (service capacity); ρ < 1 is required for stability and short waits.  
- Service layout (dedicated, pooled, super‑server) changes perceived and actual waiting times even with equal total capacity.  
- Psychological cues (lane visibility, staff consistency) can outweigh pure capacity.  
- Staffing, machine capacity, and pricing must be aligned with realistic demand and COVID‑related constraints.

## Little’s Law
> [!info] **Formulas**  
- \(L_q = \lambda \times W_q\) average queue length  
- \(L_s = \lambda \times W_s\) average in‑service count  
- \(L = L_q + L_s\) total in system  
- \(W = W_q + W_s\) total time in system  

- Use the law to move between **arrival rate (λ)**, **average time (W)**, and **average number (L)** for queue, service, or whole system.

## Utilization & Processing Time
> [!info] **Utilization** \( \rho = \dfrac{\lambda}{\text{total service rate}} \)  
- Must stay **< 1**; the closer to 0, the faster the system clears.

> [!info] **Processing time per server** \(P = \dfrac{1}{\text{server rate}}\)  
- Example: 5 c/h → \(P = 0.2 h\).  
- System‑wide effective processing time \(P_{\text{system}} = \dfrac{1}{\sum \text{rates}}\).  
- Adding parallel servers halves \(P_{\text{system}}\) but **P per server stays constant**.

> [!warning] Do **not** treat \(P\) as decreasing for each individual server when you add more servers; only the combined rate changes.

## Service Configurations (Worked Example)
> [!example] **Two markets, each 4 c/h**; three layouts:

| Layout | Servers | Total rate (c/h) | Utilization ρ | Avg waiting \(W_q\) (h) |
|--------|---------|------------------|---------------|------------------------|
| A – dedicated (2 × 5) | 2 (one per market) | 10 | 0.8 (4/5) | 0.8 |
| B – pooled (2 × 5) | 2 (shared) | 10 | 0.7 (8/10) | 0.6 |
| C – super‑server (10) | 1 (10 c/h) | 10 | 0.4 (4/10) | 0.4 |

- All have the same **arrival rate** (4 c/h per market) and **total capacity** (10 c/h).  
- Differences arise from **how work is organized** and resulting **ρ**.

## Design Take‑aways
- Apply Little’s Law to compute any missing metric (λ, W, L).  
- Compare **arrival rate** vs. **total service rate**; aim for **ρ ≤ 0.7** for comfortable buffers.  
- **Dedicated servers** improve perceived service for each segment; **pooled** or **super‑server** achieve similar performance if total capacity matches demand.  
- When utilization is identical, layout that allows **customer switching** (pooling) reduces psychological wait.

## Psychological & Layout Factors
- Customers judge wait by the **lane they see**, not total system length.  
- **Depth‑first (pooled) layout** → lower perceived queue than single line.  
- **Moving‑choice layout** (customers can switch lines) yields the best overall perception.  
- Express/priority lanes act as **pooling modifiers**; without them, all customers share one queue.  
- Visual cues (mirrors, clear signage) improve perceived progress more than clocks.

## Staffing, Machine Capacity & Pricing (Video‑business case)
- **Staff allocation**:  
  - Step 1 – full‑time (8 h) → production.  
  - Step 2 – full‑time (8 h) → customer service.  
  - One extra hire covers remaining tasks; total 8 h each day meets current load.  
- **Capacity upgrade**: To hit **H1 target (3 customers)**, add **2 machines**; evaluate cost vs. higher‑quality single machine.  
- **Stage insight**: Stage 3 needs only 4 h, freeing capacity for other work.  
- **Pricing**: Price = 577 yields equal profit; **price‑to‑volume ratio** guides whether to push volume or margin.  
- **Influence factors**: $5 factor dominates over $7 factor in the decision matrix.  
- **Training machine** (2012 model) must be **explicitly excluded** if not used; omission incurs penalty.

## Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Little’s Law | Relates arrival rate, average time, and average number in a system (L = λ·W). |
| Utilization (ρ) | Ratio of arrival rate to total service capacity; must stay < 1. |
| Processing time (P) | Inverse of a server’s service rate; per‑server constant. |
| Pooling | Allowing customers to switch among servers, effectively sharing capacity. |
| Psychological queue | Perceived wait based on visible lane length, staff consistency, and visual cues. |
