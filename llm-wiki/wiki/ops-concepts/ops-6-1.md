> [!summary] Key takeaways
- Forecasting links demand/supply data to capacity, HR, budgeting, and strategic decisions.  
- Choose a method (SMA, WMA, exponential smoothing, AI) based on data window and α that minimizes error.  
- Evaluate models with MAD, MSE, or MAE; pick the lowest‑error approach.  
- External signals help but must be combined with stable historical patterns.  
- Accurate forecasts underpin risk management, investment justification, and long‑term planning.  

## 1. Purpose & Scope of Forecasting
- Predict future demand & supply → create contingency, capacity, and financial plans.  
- Supports: raw‑material procurement, machinery sizing, labor staffing, marketing expansion, investor communication.  
- Uncertainty in markets makes systematic forecasting essential for operational stability.  

## 2. Core Forecasting Methods
> [!info] **Simple Moving Average (SMA)**
- Average of the last *n* periods, equal weight (e.g., 3‑period SMA = (Dₜ₋₂+Dₜ₋₁+Dₜ)/3).

> [!info] **Weighted Moving Average (WMA)**
- Recent periods receive higher weights; weights sum to 1 (e.g., 0.5, 0.3, 0.2 for last three periods).

> [!info] **Exponential Smoothing**
- Forecastₜ₊₁ = α·Actualₜ + (1‑α)·Forecastₜ  
- α ∈ [0,1]; α ≈ 0 trusts past forecast, α ≈ 1 trusts latest observation.  
- Industry‑published α values exist; typical α ≤ 0.5.

> [!warning] **AI / Big‑Data Models**
- Offer powerful pattern detection but can hallucinate or mis‑fit if context changes.  
- Must be validated against historical error metrics before operational use.  

### Method selection checklist
| Criterion | SMA | WMA | Exponential Smoothing | AI / Big‑Data |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Data window length | Fixed *n* | Fixed *n* with emphasis | No fixed window needed | Large historical + external |
| Responsiveness to recent change | Low | Medium | Adjustable via α | Depends on model |
| Implementation effort | Minimal | Slightly higher | Moderate (α tuning) | High (model training) |
| Typical use case | Stable demand | Gradual trend shifts | Mixed stability & trend | Complex, non‑linear patterns |

## 3. Error Metrics & Model Validation
> [!info] **Mean Absolute Deviation (MAD)**
- Average of |Actual – Forecast|; treats all errors equally.

> [!info] **Mean Squared Error (MSE)**
- Average of (Actual – Forecast)²; penalizes large deviations.

> [!info] **Mean Absolute Error (MAE)**
- Same as MAD; often reported for comparability.

- **Model selection rule**: Choose the method with the smallest average error over a validation horizon (e.g., 2‑year rolling window).  
- Use at least two years of historic data for error calculation.  
- When extending forecasts (e.g., 5‑year plan), continue the “red line” of the chosen model and adjust for required service levels (e.g., add 5 % buffer for 80 % reliability).  

> [!example] **Google‑trend vs. sales (2005‑2009)**
- Strong positive correlation; after 2009 correlation weakened due to search‑behavior shifts.  
- Lesson: external signals must be re‑validated periodically.  

> [!example] **Electricity usage forecasting**
- Quarterly household usage + 5‑year market‑share forecasts guide fuel procurement and grid storage.  
- Long‑term (40‑year) trend stability informs strategic capacity narratives.  

## 4. Operational Applications
- **Capacity Planning**: Align plant size, storage, and fuel contracts with forecasted demand.  
- **Human‑Resource Planning**: Schedule training, staffing, and project allocation based on projected workload.  
- **Budgeting & Investment**: Tie revenue projections and cost estimates to forecasted volumes; justify capital to shareholders.  
- **Risk Management**: Identify worst‑case demand scenarios; set safety stocks and financial buffers.  

## 5. Practical Tips & Common Pitfalls
- Start with the simplest method (SMA) and only move to more complex models if error reduction justifies added complexity.  
- When α is unknown, test a range (0.1‑0.5) and pick the value yielding lowest MAE/MSE.  
- Regularly re‑evaluate external data sources; correlation can decay (as seen with Google trends).  
- Document the chosen α, window size, and error metric for auditability and future model updates.  
- Avoid “focus error” by aligning forecast targets with realistic operational capabilities; use MAD to keep target stability.  

## Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| α (alpha) | Smoothing factor in exponential smoothing (0 = full reliance on past forecast, 1 = full reliance on latest observation). |
| SMA | Simple Moving Average; equal‑weight average of recent periods. |
| WMA | Weighted Moving Average; recent periods weighted more heavily. |
| MAD | Mean Absolute Deviation; average absolute forecast error. |
| MSE | Mean Squared Error; average squared forecast error, penalizing large deviations. |
| Red line | The chosen forecast trajectory extended into future periods for planning. |
| Focus error | Gap between a set focus value and the actual measured value; minimized via appropriate error metric. |
