> [!summary] Key takeaways  
- Quality = **design** (features) + **performance** (conformance to specs); they often trade off.  
- **Cost of quality** = Prevention → Appraisal → Internal failures → External failures; prevention is cheapest.  
- SPC uses **process control** then **process capability** to chase zero defects.  
- **Variation**: eliminate assignable (special) causes first; then reduce common‑cause variation.  
- Control charts (X‑bar & R) detect out‑of‑control signals; limits are ±3σ (≈99.7 % of stable data).

## 1. Quality dimensions
- **Design quality** – features, aesthetics, functionality that meet customer expectations.  
- **Performance quality** – how well the product fulfills the design specs (conformance).  
> [!info] **Conformance** – product output matches the design specification; the operations focus.  
- Trade‑off examples:  
  - High‑end restaurant bottle vs. cheap bottle – higher design quality, lower performance cost.  
  - BMW (premium design) vs. Japanese car (reliable performance) – superior feel vs. higher repair costs.  
- High‑design products can incur large **failure costs** (expensive warranty repairs).

## 2. Cost of quality
| Category | What it covers |
|----------|----------------|
| **Prevention** | Design reviews, training, process improvement |
| **Appraisal** | Inspection, testing, measurement |
| **Internal failure** | Scrap, rework, downtime before delivery |
| **External failure** | Warranty claims, recalls, reputation damage |

> [!warning] Relying only on inspection (appraisal) is insufficient; defects will slip through.  
> [!example] Apple samples incoming components; only those meeting specs enter full production.  
- Real‑world failure costs: luxury‑car repairs, global automotive recalls, smartphone warranty service.  
- **Prevention > appraisal > failures** – investing early reduces total cost and boosts reliability.

## 3. SPC fundamentals
- **Zero‑defect mindset** = prevent defects at the source, not detect them later.  
> [!info] **Process‑focused quality** – design the process so it cannot produce defects; contrasts with product‑focused inspection.  
- SPC two‑step workflow:  
  1. **Process control** – verify statistical stability (in‑control).  
  2. **Process capability** – assess ability to meet customer specs.  
- **Variation types**

| Type | Source | Typical pattern |
|------|--------|-----------------|
| **Common‑cause** (random) | Inherent to the process | Normal distribution, μ ± σ |
| **Assignable** (special) | Specific, identifiable factors (e.g., operator error) | Outliers, trends, shifts |

> [!warning] Ignoring assignable causes may keep the process “in‑spec” but will eventually generate defects.  
- Central Limit Theorem: sample means are normally distributed; a stable process yields ≈99.7 % of points within μ ± 3σ.  
- Example: Target weight 45 g → 99.7 % should fall between 44 g and 46 g.

## 4. Control chart construction
### Steps to build X‑bar & R charts
1. Collect a large lot (≈2 000 units).  
2. Randomly sample ~100‑120 units; split into sub‑groups of **n = 4‑6**.  
3. For each sub‑group compute **X̄** (mean) and **R** (range).  
4. Calculate overall **X̄̄** (average of X̄) and **R̄** (average of R).  
5. Use constants (A₂, D₃, D₄) from the standard table to set limits:  

| Limit | Formula |
|-------|---------|
| **UCL (X‑bar)** | X̄̄ + A₂·R̄ |
| **LCL (X‑bar)** | X̄̄ − A₂·R̄ |
| **UCL (R)** | D₄·R̄ |
| **LCL (R)** | D₃·R̄ |

> [!info] **A₂** converts average range to an estimate of σ; values depend on n (e.g., A₂ = 0.729 for n = 4).  
> [!example] With n = 4, R̄ = 2.16, D₃ = 0, D₄ = 2.28 → R‑chart limits: LCL = 0, UCL = 4.92.  

- **Interpretation**  
  - **In‑control**: all subgroup means and ranges lie within their respective limits.  
  - **Out‑of‑control**: any point outside limits → investigate assignable cause (setup error, tool wear, material issue).  
  - Even if all points are within limits, a systematic trend may still warrant investigation.  

> [!warning] Small subgroup sizes (< 4) or too few sub‑groups (< 25) weaken the CLT assumption; limits become unreliable.  

## 5. Managing variation
- **Step 1:** Detect & eliminate assignable causes (special‑cause) using control charts.  
- **Step 2:** Reduce common‑cause variation through standard work, training, and robust process design.  
- Japanese philosophy: aim for **100 % performance** → lower overall cost, reinforce zero‑defect goal.  

## Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Design quality** | Features and functionality that meet customer expectations |
| **Performance quality** | Conformance of output to design specifications |
| **Cost of quality** | Sum of prevention, appraisal, internal and external failure costs |
| **Zero‑defect mindset** | Prevent defects at the source rather than detect them later |
| **Common‑cause variation** | Random, inherent process variation (normal distribution) |
| **Assignable (special‑cause) variation** | Specific, identifiable source of variation |
| **Control limits** | Statistically derived ±3σ bounds for a stable process |
| **Process capability** | Ability of a stable process to meet customer specs |
