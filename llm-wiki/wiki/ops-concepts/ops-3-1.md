<div class="dc-callout warn">System throughput = effective capacity of the bottleneck</div>

> [!summary] Key takeaways
> - Flow = min(demand, capacity); bottleneck sets system capacity.  
> - Utilization = flow / design capacity; low utilization hides true capacity.  
> - Reduce upstream yield loss or elevate the bottleneck to raise throughput.  
> - Break‑even: Fixed / (contribution margin) → required sales volume.  
> - Apply the 5‑step TOC cycle continuously.

## Capacity, Demand & Bottleneck

> [!info] **Demand vs. Supply**  
> Flow rate = min(external demand, internal supply).

> [!info] **Bottleneck**  
> The stage whose effective capacity (after yield loss) limits the whole system.

> [!example]  
> - Step 1: 2 min/unit → 30 units/h design capacity.  
> - 40 % yield loss → effective output = 30 × 0.60 = 18 units/h.  
> - Step 1 becomes the bottleneck; system capacity = 18 units/h.

> [!warning] **Common mistake** – adding capacities of all steps (e.g., 30 + 30 + 60) gives a meaningless “average” capacity.

## Utilization

> [!info] **Utilization formula**  
> Util = (flow ÷ design capacity) = fraction or %.

| Machine | Flow (units/min) | Design capacity (units/min) | Utilization |
|---------|------------------|-----------------------------|-------------|
| A       | 1/10             | 1/8                         | 80 % |
| B       | 1/9              | 1/9                         | 100 % |

> [!example]  
> If a bottleneck runs at 18 units/h and other steps can run faster, overall utilization = 18 / 30 = 60 %.

## Yield and Its Effect

> [!info] **Yield** – proportion of good units out of total processed (quality metric).  
> **Yield loss** reduces effective capacity of the step where it occurs.

> [!example]  
> 10 % loss at step 1 (30 → 27 units/h) does **not** affect system output if step 2 (capacity = 20 units/h) remains the bottleneck. Larger loss would lower step 2’s effective capacity.

## Five‑Step Improvement (Theory of Constraints)

1. **Identify** the bottleneck.  
2. **Exploit** – keep it running continuously (no idle time).  
3. **Subordinate** – align all other processes to the bottleneck’s pace.  
4. **Elevate** – add resources or improve technology at the bottleneck.  
5. **Repeat** – a new bottleneck will emerge.

> [!warning] Ignoring step 2 (subordination) causes upstream or downstream queues that waste capacity.

## Cost Concepts

> [!info] **Fixed cost** – incurred regardless of output (e.g., lease, franchise fee).  
> **Variable (marginal) cost** – cost per additional unit (ingredients, royalties).  
> **Total cost** = Fixed + (Variable × Quantity).

> [!example] Subway store  
> - Fixed ≈ $150 k (lease, franchise fee, equipment).  
> - Variable ≈ $2 per sandwich (ingredients + royalties).  
> - Selling price ≈ $7 → contribution margin = $5 per unit.  
> - Break‑even quantity = $150 k / $5 ≈ 30 000 sandwiches.

## Break‑Even Analysis

> [!info] **Formula**  
> \[
> Q^{*}= \frac{\text{Fixed Cost}}{\text{Price per unit} - \text{Variable Cost}}
> \]

> [!example]  
> Fixed = $240 k, Price = $7, Variable = $2.5 → Margin = $4.5 → Q* ≈ 53 333 units.

> [!warning] Mixing % (e.g., royalty %) with absolute unit costs leads to calculation errors; convert all terms to the same monetary unit before solving.

## Operational Insights

- Higher utilization spreads fixed costs over more units, lowering per‑unit cost.  
- When capacity is constrained and demand is unlimited, throughput equals bottleneck capacity.  
- Adding parallel machines at the bottleneck increases total capacity linearly (Σ individual capacities).  
- Consistent units (units/h or units/min) are essential for accurate capacity calculations.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Bottleneck** | Stage that determines the maximum system throughput. |
| **Utilization** | Ratio of actual flow to design capacity (fraction or %). |
| **Yield** | Percentage of good units out of total processed. |
| **Contribution margin** | Price − Variable cost; amount that covers fixed cost and profit. |
| **Break‑even quantity (Q*)** | Output level where total revenue equals total cost. |
| **5‑Step TOC** | Identify, Exploit, Subordinate, Elevate, Repeat – continuous improvement cycle. |
