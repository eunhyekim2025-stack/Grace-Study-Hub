> [!summary] Key takeaways  
- Know your BATNA, reservation price, and target price before any negotiation.  
- First offers anchor outcomes; use them strategically.  
- Distributive = split‑the‑pie; Integrative = expand the pie.  
- Leadership = influencing toward a shared goal; emergence ≠ effectiveness.  
- Transformational leadership’s 4 I’s drive high performance **and** follower satisfaction.

## Negotiation Fundamentals

> [!info] **BATNA** – Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement; gives you power to walk away.  
> [!info] **Reservation price** – lowest acceptable outcome; never disclose.  
> [!info] **Target price** – most ambitious outcome you aim for.  

- **Anchoring**: the first offer pulls the final agreement toward it (positive correlation).  
- **Preparation checklist**:  
  - Identify own & counterpart’s BATNA.  
  - Set reservation & target prices.  
  - Quantify measurable prospects; note non‑quantifiable factors.  
  - Strengthen your BATNA where possible.  
- **Package deals**: present multiple, equally‑valued bundles → reveals counterpart preferences without exposing your own priorities.  

### Negotiation Types

| Type | Pie nature | Typical outcome |
|------|------------|-----------------|
| Distributive (zero‑sum) | Fixed | One party’s gain = other’s loss |
| Integrative (win‑win) | Expandable | Both parties can improve |

> [!warning] Over‑aggressive tactics in niche job markets can damage long‑term reputation; always ask “what is actually negotiable?” first.

## Leadership Foundations

> [!info] **Leadership** – the process of influencing people toward a shared goal; can be formal (assigned) or informal (emergent).  

- **Effectiveness criteria**:  
  1. Goal achievement (performance).  
  2. Follower satisfaction & motivation (sustainability).  
  3. Avoidance of short‑term success that burns out followers.  

- **Emergence vs Effectiveness**:  
  - *Emergence* driven by traits matching stereotypical leader prototypes (extraversion, conscientiousness, emotional stability).  
  - *Effectiveness* depends on skills, ethical stance, and context; traits are weaker predictors.  

- **Ethics matter**: charisma & mobilization (e.g., Hitler) are insufficient without moral character.

## Leadership Styles & Outcomes (Class Exercise)

| Style | Core behavior | Typical context | Observed satisfaction |
|-------|---------------|----------------|-----------------------|
| Transformational | Inspire, role‑model, hands‑on | Change‑driven, high‑impact | Highest |
| Democratic‑participative | Facilitate discussion, final decision authority | Complex, creative tasks | High |
| Autocratic (directive) | Single‑expert commands | High‑stress, time‑pressured | Low |
| Laissez‑faire | Full autonomy to experts | Academic, low‑power‑distance | Mixed |

> [!example] In the “crane factory” activity, Team 3 (democratic style) won with 12 cranes and reported the highest team vibe.

## Transformational Leadership – The Four “I’s”

1. **Idealized Influence** – role model, earns admiration.  
2. **Inspirational Motivation** – clear, compelling vision.  
3. **Intellectual Stimulation** – encourages curiosity & innovation.  
4. **Individualized Consideration** – tailors coaching to each follower.  

> [!warning] Charismatic leaders can manipulate followers (e.g., Hitler); ethical grounding is essential.

## Followers & Movement Dynamics

- **First follower** turns a lone “nut” into a leader; makes the movement visible.  
- **Second follower** shifts perception from isolated act to emerging crowd.  
- **Three‑plus followers** create a “crowd” that attracts more participants; risk of ridicule for non‑joiners drops.  
- **Key stats**: ~50 % of leadership effectiveness stems from cultivating followers; 60‑80 % of emergence variance is environmental.  

> [!info] Co‑leadership (e.g., Jobs + Wozniak) balances complementary strengths and mitigates individual weaknesses.

## Practical Takeaways for Leaders

- Be courageous as the first follower; make participation easy and visible.  
- Nurture early supporters as equals; keep focus on the cause, not personal fame.  
- Adapt style to context: high‑power‑distance may need clear direction; low‑power‑distance benefits from autonomy.  
- Develop leadership skills through training, practice, and feedback—traits alone are insufficient.

## Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| BATNA | Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement; your walk‑away option |
| Anchoring | Psychological effect where the first offer pulls the final agreement toward it |
| Distributive negotiation | Fixed‑pie, zero‑sum bargaining |
| Integrative negotiation | Win‑win, expands the value pool |
| Transformational leadership | Style built on the 4 I’s; drives high performance & satisfaction |
| Leadership emergence | Process by which a person becomes a leader, often trait‑driven |
| Leadership effectiveness | How well a leader achieves goals and satisfies followers |
| Power distance | Degree to which less‑powerful members accept unequal power distribution |
