> [!summary] Key takeaways
- Classify negotiations (distributive, compatible, integrated) to choose the right approach.  
- Prepare: know your BATNA, reservation, target, and estimate the counterpart’s priorities.  
- Use package‑deal, NASALs, and generous “TipperTie” moves to expand the pie and uncover hidden preferences.  
- Disclose gradually; share interests, not limits, and match opponent’s openness.  
- Keep 3‑4 strong alternatives to preserve leverage and protect relationships.  

## 1. Negotiation Types
- **Distributive** – fixed‑pie, zero‑sum; one party’s gain is the other’s loss.  
- **Compatible** – parties share the same interest on an issue; easy agreement.  
- **Integrated** – interests differ; value can be expanded by trading off issues.  
> [!info] Most real‑world negotiations are compatible or integrated; pure distributive cases are rare.

## 2. Preparation Checklist
| Item | Why it matters |
|------|----------------|
| BATNA | Determines your walk‑away power. |
| Reservation price | Lowest acceptable outcome; never reveal it. |
| Target price | Desired outcome; guides concessions. |
| Counterpart’s BATNA & priorities | Enables strategic trade‑offs. |
> [!warning] Disclosing your reservation or red‑line gives the opponent leverage; share only interests.

## 3. Strategic Tactics
- **TipperTie (generous first move)** – start with a concession; if the other side misreads, respond with a goodwill gesture.  
- **Generous TipperTie** – add occasional forgiveness for perceived bad actions.  
- **Disclosure ladder**: begin with minimal openness; raise level only when the counterpart reciprocates.  
- **Multiple Equivalent Offers (NASALs)** – present 2‑3 bundles of equal value to you; opponent’s choice reveals true priorities.  
- **Package‑deal negotiation** – bundle issues, test which combination the counterpart prefers; avoids the “midpoint” trap of issue‑by‑issue talks.  

> [!example] In the Drop‑Tech case, trading commission points for the spokesperson role (integrated issues) added ~1,000 points beyond the midpoint solution.

## 4. Scoring & Packaging
- Assign points to every issue (including qualitative factors) to compare offers objectively.  
- Example scoring table:

| Issue | Points (your valuation) |
|-------|--------------------------|
| Location | 90 |
| Rent (3,000 $) | 70 |
| Parking | 15 |
| ... | ... |

- Total scores let you rank multiple listings or job offers.  

> [!info] Quantify “unquantifiable” items (e.g., brand exposure) to avoid over‑weighting purely numeric factors.

## 5. Practical Applications
### Job offers
- Ask “what is negotiable?” to surface hidden levers (vacation, equipment, remote days).  
- Protect future relationships; avoid appearing self‑serving in every meeting.  

### Real‑estate rentals
- Keep cash ready for a strong fallback.  
- Maintain **3‑4 equally attractive alternatives** to avoid being forced into a bad price.  
- Understand landlord/agent incentives (speed vs. commission) to shape offers.  

### Service contracts (e.g., wedding planners)
- Request itemized quotes from each vendor.  
- Compare line items, ask for explanations, and verify market prices to spot overcharging.  

> [!warning] Vendors often embed “standard” fees; scrutinize every line item.

## Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| BATNA | Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement – your fallback option. |
| Integrated negotiation | Situation where parties have differing interests and can create extra value through trade‑offs. |
| NASALs | Multiple Equivalent Offers; presenting several equal‑value packages to elicit opponent preferences. |
| TipperTie | Cooperation strategy that starts with a generous concession and includes forgiveness for misread signals. |
| Midpoint approach | Issue‑by‑issue compromise that often leaves value on the table. |
| Pareto‑efficient | Outcome where no party can be better off without making the other worse off. |
