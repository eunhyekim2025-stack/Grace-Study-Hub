> [!summary] Key takeaways
> - Individual biases (overconfidence, illusion of control, sunk‑cost) drive poor choices; awareness and tiny incentives improve accuracy.  
> - High power distance speeds action but blocks information flow; leaders can counteract by speaking last and fostering psychological safety.  
> - Treat organizations as open systems: map causal chains, run pre‑mortems, use nudges and commitment devices to steer behavior.  

## 1. Individual decision biases  

- **Overconfidence bias** – systematic over‑estimation of one’s abilities; e.g., 93 % of drivers think they’re better than most.  
> [!info] **Overconfidence bias** – People systematically overestimate their abilities and think they are above the median.  

- **Illusion of control** – belief that random outcomes are controllable; manifests in superstitions and “magical” actions.  
> [!info] **Illusion of control** – Perceived ability to influence truly random events.  

- **Sunk‑cost fallacy / escalation of commitment** – continued investment in a failing plan because of past resources.  
> [!info] **Sunk‑cost fallacy** – Tendency to keep investing in a losing venture due to prior expenditures.  

- **Self‑serving bias** – attributing successes to personal skill and failures to external factors (mentioned as a key individual bias).  

### Mitigation at the individual level  
- Small monetary incentives (cents per correct answer) raise factual accuracy.  
- Nudges: default options, clear labeling, visual cues (red/green).  
- Explicit, repeatable feedback: tell people what they do well and where to improve.  
- Training for self‑ and interpersonal awareness (e.g., “Bad News” game).  

> [!warning] Managers often overestimate how clearly employees understand feedback; transparent communication is essential.  

## 2. Group‑level influences  

### Power distance  
- **Definition** – extent to which unequal power distribution is accepted.  
> [!info] **Power distance** – Cultural acceptance of hierarchical authority; low in Northern Europe/NA, high in East Asia, Russia, Southern regions.  

- High power distance can enable rapid, disciplined action but harms information sharing and psychological safety.  
- Empirical link: 36 k expedition participants showed higher mortality in high‑power‑distance groups.  

### Mitigating excessive power distance  
- Leaders speak **last** in meetings to encourage honest input.  
- Build psychological safety so dissent is welcomed.  
- Example: Joseph Sano’s struggle to challenge entrenched views illustrates the cost of suppression.  

### Transactive memory & collective intelligence  
- Shared knowledge of “who knows what” improves problem‑solving performance.  
> [!info] **Transactive memory** – A system where team members know each other’s expertise, enabling fast knowledge retrieval.  

## 3. System‑level perspective  

- **Closed vs open systems** – closed: fixed rules (e.g., checkers); open: ambiguous, evolving conditions (most organizations).  
> [!info] **Open system** – Environment with changing rules and interdependent components; decisions have cascading effects.  

- **Path dependence** – early choices lock future options (e.g., single vendor lock‑in).  

- **Pre‑mortem** – imagine worst‑case outcome, list causes, design safeguards.  

- **Nudges & defaults** – rearranging choice architecture, pre‑selected options, visual cues steer behavior without restricting freedom.  

- **Commitment devices** – public pledges, financial penalties, app blockers, rounding‑up savings reinforce goals.  

- **Risk‑aware stance** – expect failures, adopt pessimistic planning, use independent audits for outsider checks.  

## 4. Practical toolbox  

| Tool | How it works | Typical impact |
|------|--------------|----------------|
| Small incentives | Pay a few cents per correct answer | ↑ factual accuracy |
| Default options | Pre‑select desired choice | ↑ adoption rates |
| Visual cues (red/green) | Signal desirability instantly | Faster decisions |
| Public pledge | Announce goal on social media | Social pressure → compliance |
| Financial penalty | Lose pledged money if target missed | Strong motivation |
| App blockers | Restrict distracting sites for set time | ↑ focus |
| Rounding‑up savings | Save change from each transaction | Cumulative wealth building |
| Pre‑mortem | List worst‑case causes before project | Early risk identification |
| Independent audit | External review of decisions | Bias detection |

## 5. Designing a *worst* decision process (class exercise)  

- **Individual:** overconfident leader ignores data, assumes personal superiority, refuses to admit error.  
- **Group:** consensus‑driven team, dominant overconfident member dictates, dissent punished.  
- **System:** rigid hierarchy silences lower voices, metrics force rapid launch, excessive options create analysis paralysis.  

> [!example] The scenario illustrates how anchoring on sunk costs, short‑term performance focus, and a culture that punishes questioning produce disastrous outcomes.

| Transactive memory | Shared awareness of who holds which expertise |
