## Two levels of diversity

| Level | What it is | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Surface-level** | Visible, largely immutable, seen almost instantly | ethnicity, sex, age |
| **Deep-level** | Subject to **construal** — subjective, perceptual, revealed over time | values, beliefs, attitudes |

Three questions frame the topic: **What are the opportunities of diversity? What are the challenges? How do we get the best of it?**

## Opportunity — the wisdom of crowds

> [!example] The class estimate
> Everyone privately guessed the height of a tall structure (answer ≈ **330 m**). No single guess was reliable, but the **average** guess (~250 m) landed far closer to the truth than most individuals — the deviation of the *combined* estimate is much smaller than the *average individual* deviation.

Why it works: individual errors **cancel** — some overestimate, some underestimate. For that cancellation you need:

- **Bracketing** — estimates on *both* sides of the truth (not everyone erring the same way).
- **Roughly balanced magnitude** — if the spread skews to one side, the benefit disappears.
- **Independent, diverse information** — people must contribute *different* perspectives, not copy each other.

Two error types matter: **random error** (cancels out) vs **systematic error** (a shared bias — e.g. the class guessing *low* together — which crowds *cannot* fix; think one newspaper owner skewing a whole electorate).

> [!info] Why this matters for diversity
> Diverse groups make **better, more creative decisions** on complex tasks, because they bring different information — the "whole greater than the sum of its parts" (super-additivity). It helps most when conflict is about the **task, not the relationship**.

## Challenge — when differences divide

- **Organizational demography:** too much demographic difference can **reduce social cohesion**.
- **Structural holes:** the network splinters into parts that stop talking to each other — **echo chambers / silos**.
- **Faultlines:** hypothetical dividing lines where *several attributes line up* to split a group into subgroups. Surface-level attributes (age, ethnicity, gender) create them early; deep-level ones (values, background) matter more later.

> [!warning] Faultline risk is highest at *medium* diversity
> A homogeneous team has no line to split on (low risk). A *very* diverse team also has low risk — "if everyone's different, no one's different," so no clean subgroups form. The danger is **medium** diversity, where attributes align into two coherent camps (e.g. a group of dancers vs a group of teachers of similar ages).

## What drives faultlines (the mechanisms)

- **Similarity–attraction:** we are drawn to people like us — which quietly biases hiring toward sameness (homophily).
- **Social identity theory:** we self-categorize into groups to belong and feel esteem, then think in **in-group vs out-group** terms. The **minimal group paradigm** shows this is arbitrary — assign strangers to a "red" or "blue" team and they immediately favour their own and show hostility to the other, despite the split being meaningless.
- **Social comparison → discrimination:** comparing ourselves to others highlights differences and leads to categorizing (and stereotyping) people.

> [!note] Diversity also *signals*
> Visible representation carries meaning: if a company's top leadership is, say, 98% men, that alone signals to women whether the opportunity is real — demographic diversity communicates values before anyone speaks.

## How to get the best of diversity

## Key takeaways

- Diversity is a **group-level** trait with **surface** (visible) and **deep** (values) levels.
- **Opportunity:** the *wisdom of crowds* — independent, bracketing, diverse estimates cancel errors and beat individuals (but not **systematic** bias).
- **Challenge:** **faultlines**, **structural holes/silos**, and **social identity** ("us vs them") — with risk highest at **medium** diversity.
- **Manage it** with **psychological safety, a superordinate identity, common goals, and positive contact.**

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Surface- vs deep-level diversity** | Visible/immutable attributes vs values, beliefs, attitudes |
| **Wisdom of crowds** | Averaging independent, diverse estimates cancels random error and beats most individuals |
| **Bracketing** | Estimates falling on both sides of the truth — needed for errors to cancel |
| **Systematic error** | A shared bias in one direction that crowds cannot correct |
| **Faultline** | A dividing line where aligned attributes split a group into subgroups |
| **Structural hole** | A gap where parts of a network stop communicating (silo/echo chamber) |
| **Social identity theory** | We categorize into in-groups/out-groups to belong — the root of "us vs them" |
| **Superordinate identity** | A shared higher-order group that unites subgroups |
| **Contact theory** | Positive contact across differences reduces prejudice |
