> [!summary] Key takeaways  
- Teams succeed when roles are clear, contributions visible, and status cues managed.  
- Moderate task conflict boosts performance; relationship conflict and social loafing hurt it.  
- Persuasion works via seven principles; use ethically and align with audience identity.  
- Conformity drops with private responses, allies, or broken unanimity.  
- Framing (gain vs. loss) and defaults steer choices with minimal effort.

## 1. Team & Group Foundations
- **Group** = information‑sharing; members individually accountable.  
- **Team** = synergy‑oriented; collective accountability, performance focus.  
> [!info] **Process gain** – team output > average of individual scores.  
> [!warning] **Process loss** – redundancy, dominance, or loafing erodes gain.  

### Development Stages (Tuckman)
| Stage | Core activity |
|-------|---------------|
| Forming | Getting acquainted |
| Storming | Resolving disagreements |
| Norming | Establishing norms |
| Performing | Efficient execution |
| Adjourning | Disbanding after goal |

> [!example] **Temporary‑team curve** – slow start → mid‑term panic → sprint finish (e.g., semester project).

### Status Cues & Dominance
- Assertiveness, confidence, talkativeness → perceived dominance → skewed idea uptake.  
- **Social dominance** → passive members, reduced input.

## 2. Conflict, Cohesion, & Social Loafing
- **Task (process) conflict** – moderate level improves ideas; too little → premature consensus; too much → stall.  
- **Relationship conflict** – almost always harms performance.  
> [!warning] **Common‑knowledge problem** – re‑discussing known info wastes time, blocks process gain.  

- **Social loafing** – effort drops when individual contribution is invisible or when dominant voices dominate.  
- **Cohesion** amplifies existing norms: positive if norms support performance, negative if they reinforce counter‑productive attitudes.  
- **Group size** – larger groups ↑ loafing, diffusion of responsibility.  

> [!info] **Goal‑setting rule** – clear, transparent goals reduce loafing; vague large goals increase it.  

- **Hawthorne effect** – observation changes behavior; peer evaluation can boost participation but may create toxic pressure.

## 3. Persuasion Principles (Seven)
| Principle | Core idea | Typical cue |
|-----------|----------|-------------|
| Reciprocity | Return a favor/gift | Small humor, favor |
| Scarcity | Rare items seem more valuable | Limited‑edition |
| Authority | Follow credible experts | Doctor endorsement |
| Consistency | Align with prior actions/values | Small initial favor |
| Liking | Likeable source → compliance | Warm humor |
| Social Proof | Follow majority behavior | Influencer endorsement |
| Unity | Shared group identity boosts influence | In‑group spokesperson |

> [!example] **Phone‑number request** – contextual/reciprocal (humor, small favor) → higher acceptance vs. blunt direct ask.  

- Ethical use: aim for genuine help, not manipulation; aligns teams and customers.

## 4. Conformity & Social Influence
### Classic Asch Findings
- **Unanimous confederates** → ~37 % conformity on critical trials.  
- **One ally** (correct partner) → conformity drops to ~5 %.  
- **Private response** (written) → conformity reduced by two‑thirds.  

> [!info] **Informational conformity** – belief group is correct.  
> [!info] **Normative conformity** – desire to avoid disapproval.  

### Key Modulators
| Factor | Effect on conformity |
|--------|----------------------|
| Unanimity | Strongest driver |
| Presence of ally | Weakens pressure sharply |
| Public vs. private response | Public ↑, private ↓ |
| Task difficulty | ↑ conformity when judgment is hard |
| Group size | ↑ conformity when large and visible |

> [!example] **Hidden‑camera norm** – woman learns behavior from others, continues alone, then spreads it as a new norm.  

### Decision‑making Implications
- Use **private voting** to limit unwanted conformity.  
- Deploy **public voting** when consensus is desired.  
- For high‑stakes choices, prefer **unanimous or super‑majority** rules.

## 5. Framing Effects & Defaults
- **Gain frame** (“save jobs”) → risk‑averse, certainty‑seeking.  
- **Loss frame** (“lose jobs”) → risk‑seeking, higher engagement.  
> [!example] Blood‑donation appeal: loss‑framed message doubled show‑up rates.  

- **Defaults** act as implicit suggestions; inertia keeps users in the default path.  
- Effective in subscriptions, policy settings, spectrum allocation.

> [!warning] Over‑reliance on defaults can limit autonomy; ensure opt‑out is easy.

## 6. Applied Cases
- **Jury deliberation** – early majority vote sways later votes (conformity pressure).  
- **Phone‑number experiment** – reciprocity & liking increase compliance.  
- **Film “Top 100 Men”** – illustrates structural (voting rules) and interpersonal influence tactics in a closed‑room setting.

## Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Process gain | Team output exceeds average individual output |
| Social loafing | Reduced effort when individual contribution is hidden |
| Reciprocity | Obligation to return a favor or gift |
| Informational conformity | Conforming because group seems correct |
| Normative conformity | Conforming to avoid social disapproval |
| Framing effect | Choice alteration by presenting info as gain vs. loss |
| Default bias | Preference for the pre‑selected option due to inertia |
