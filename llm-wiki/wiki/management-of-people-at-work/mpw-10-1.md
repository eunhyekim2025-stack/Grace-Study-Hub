> [!summary] Key takeaways  
- Effective leadership = performance + follower experience + sustainability.  
- Power grows from others’ dependence; coalitions, self‑promotion, ingratiation boost it.  
- Strong culture = widely shared + intensely held; drives behavior, performance, but can create coercion and rigidity.  
- Policy fit (e.g., Amazon vs Spotify) must match industry work‑type; visibility matters for junior staff.  
- Bridgewater’s radical truth & algorithmic “believability” illustrate a high‑intensity culture that fuels performance.

## 1. Leadership concepts  

- **Green‑Map Theory** warns against “great‑person” narratives that over‑emphasize single leaders.  
- **Leadership emergence** ≠ **effective emergence** – formal appointment doesn’t guarantee influence.  
- **Effective leadership** requires balancing three components:  

> [!info] **Leadership Balance** – performance, follower experience, sustainability must all be addressed.  

- **Styles & trade‑offs**  

| Style | Strengths | Risks |
|-------|-----------|-------|
| Transformational | Inspires vision, boosts performance & satisfaction | Unethical behavior, cult‑like followership, personality worship |
| Democratic/Participative | High satisfaction, fits skilled autonomous teams | May lack direction when rapid decisions needed |
| Directive/Authoritarian | Quick short‑term results | Damages follower experience, harms long‑term sustainability |

- **First follower** (TED Talk) – legitimizes leader, models good followership, makes movement visible.  

## 2. Power dynamics  

- **Source of power**: others’ dependence on you (control of scarce, non‑substitutable resources).  
- High power effects: ↓ empathy, ↑ impulsivity, risk‑taking (“Throat Genetic Theory”).  
- **Collective power**: coalitions (e.g., unions) raise earnings & bargaining strength.  

> [!info] **Ways to increase personal power** – form coalitions, self‑promotion, ingratiation, exemplification.  

- **Power typology**  

| Type | Basis |
|------|-------|
| Formal | Position, authority |
| Informal | Influence without title |
| Coercive | Punishment |
| Reward | Incentives |
| Expert | Specialized knowledge |
| Referent/Record | Role‑model respect |
| Legitimate | Accepted authority serving common good |

- **Building power** – map internal sources, build quiet consensus, differentiate formal vs informal structures.  

## 3. Organizational culture  

- **Definition** – shared system of meaning (values, beliefs, assumptions) that shapes perception & behavior.  
- **Schein’s three layers**  

| Layer | What it is |
|-------|------------|
| Artifacts | Visible structures, dress, rituals |
| Espoused values | Stated beliefs, mission statements |
| Underlying assumptions | Deep, often unconscious beliefs |

- **Strong culture** = values **widely shared** & **intensely held**.  

> [!info] **Strong culture** – drives behavior, reduces uncertainty, aligns employees, yields financial advantage.  

- **Benefits** – faster decisions, higher commitment, less micromanagement, strategic edge.  
- **Risks** – coercion, inertia, homogeneity, exclusion of mis‑fits.  

### Cultural mechanisms  

- **Stories**, **rituals** (e.g., Sweden’s “Fika”), **symbols**, **language** convey culture.  
- Leaders reinforce via mission statements, public messaging, storytelling.  

### Company examples  

| Company | Cultural stance | Core impact |
|---------|----------------|-------------|
| Netflix | Performance‑driven meritocracy (“team, not family”) | Attracts high‑achievers; pressure & burnout risk |
| Anthropic | CEO devotes 40 % to culture; wary of short‑term incentives | Signals purpose, seeks fit beyond stock options |
| Bridgewater Associates | Radical truth & transparency; “believability” weighting | Superior investment performance; intense feedback, potential fear |

- **Bridgewater tools** – baseball cards, dot collector (real‑time rating, weighted by expertise).  

> [!example] **Dot collector** – participants rate each other 1‑10 on many traits; algorithm weights opinions by demonstrated expertise, producing merit‑based decisions.  

- **Cultural strength & performance** – empirical link to higher ROIC, revenue growth; also acts as a filter for talent fit.  

## 4. Post‑COVID work‑policy contrast  

| Firm | Policy | Underlying assumption | Fit |
|------|--------|-----------------------|-----|
| Amazon | 5 days on‑site | Need close supervision, physical co‑location for goods handling | Physical‑goods, logistics |
| Spotify | “Work from anywhere” | Knowledge work can be remote; autonomy boosts performance | Software, digital services |

- **Downsides** – perceived unfairness, social‑class divide, sub‑group silos.  
- **Advice for juniors** – office presence ↑ visibility, networking, mentorship, promotion chances.  

## 5. Cautions & warnings  

> [!warning] **Transformational leadership** – can devolve into personality worship; monitor ethical conduct.  
> [!warning] **Authoritarian style** – short‑term gains may sacrifice long‑term sustainability.  
> [!warning] **Strong culture** – may become coercive, inhibit diversity, create fear (e.g., Bridgewater surveillance reports).  

## 6. Key terms  

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Leadership emergence | Formal appointment to a leadership role |
| Effective emergence | Actual influence and impact on followers |
| Power dependence | Power derived from others’ need for scarce resources |
| Radical truth | Unvarnished, honest feedback as cultural norm |
| Believability weighting | Decision weighting based on rater’s demonstrated expertise |
| Strong culture | Values that are both widely shared and intensely held |
| First follower | Early adopter who legitimizes and models followership |
