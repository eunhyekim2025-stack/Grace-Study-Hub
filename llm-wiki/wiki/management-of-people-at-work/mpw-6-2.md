> [!summary] Key takeaways
- Secret ballots dampen peer pressure, shaping verdict outcomes.  
- Foot‑in‑door and reciprocity create low‑commitment footholds for larger compliance.  
- Unity/identity framing aligns jurors with a collective goal, reducing inter‑group conflict.  
- Physical constraints (train roar, travel distance) can expose impossible witness timelines.  
- Open voting and the “reasonable doubt” standard protect against wrongful convictions.

## 1. Secret ballot & peer pressure
- Jury moved from public voice vote to secret written ballot.  
- Tally: **10‑2** guilty; secrecy cited as factor that lowered peer pressure.  
- Challenge: “no secrets in the jury room” – tension between private voting and public accountability.

## 2. Persuasion techniques
> [!info] **Foot‑in‑door (putting the dollar)**
> Securing agreement to a small request raises compliance with a later larger request.

> [!example] *Survey‑to‑intrusive request*: Women who first completed a brief survey were **2×** more likely to let a man into their home than those who received only the intrusive request.

- **Application (Fonda case)**: Uses tentative phrasing (“it’s possible”) and invites discussion (“let’s just talk”) as a low‑commitment entry point before stronger arguments.

> [!info] **Reciprocity principle**
> Giving a small favor increases the likelihood the recipient returns the favor.

> [!example] *Soda‑raffle experiment*: Participants who received a free soda bought **more raffle tickets** than those who did not.

- Fonda’s “gamble”: sacrifices his own “road” (personal concession) to trigger reciprocal support, aiming for a decisive vote.

## 3. Witness testimony & physical plausibility
| Witness | Claim | Physical constraint | Verdict on plausibility |
|---------|-------|---------------------|------------------------|
| Elderly neighbor | Heard boy say “I’m going to kill you” **immediately** before body hit floor | Train roar ≈ 10 s drowns out speech | **Impossible** – noise would mask voice |
| Woman across street | Saw killing through last two cars of a six‑car train | Train passes a point in ≈ 10 s; visual window limited | Suggests murder occurred **after** train passed, contradicting neighbor’s timeline |
| Elderly witness (stairs) | Boy ran 55 ft, opened chain‑locked front door in **15 s** | Human sprint + door manipulation ≈ >15 s | **Questionable** – defense avoided challenging to not hurt case |

> [!warning] **Timing error**: Assuming the boy could hear or be heard during the 10‑second train pass leads to a physical impossibility, undermining the neighbor’s testimony.

- Defense argues the elderly neighbor may lie for attention (age, limp, torn jacket).  
- Prosecution concedes timing doubt but argues a shouted statement could cut through noise.

## 4. Group dynamics & deliberation tactics
> [!info] **Unity principle (Cialdini)**
> Persuasion is stronger when people see themselves as part of a shared, attractive group.

- “German watchmaker” speech:  
  1. “We are not here to fight.”  
  2. “We have a responsibility.”  
  3. “We are strong.”  
  → Frames deliberation as a **superordinate goal**, lowering “us vs. them” tension.

- Social‑identity experiment (summer‑camp teams): Distinct group names ↑ rivalry; common goal ↓ conflict.

- Jury composition: slum‑background members, immigrant watchmaker, etc., created “us vs. them” undercurrents; unity framing helped refocus on collective task.

- **Open ballot**: Votes called aloud, tallied; tie (e.g., 6‑6) triggers further discussion.

> [!info] **Reasonable doubt**
> Legal safeguard: No conviction without a level of certainty that eliminates reasonable doubt.

- Jurors debated:  
  - One cites eyewitness who saw the stabbing as decisive.  
  - Another points out possible glasses‑related misidentification (night‑time, nose‑rubbing marks).  
  - Consensus: If any credible doubt remains, conviction must be withheld.

- Interpersonal tactics observed: calling objections, prompting each member to articulate arguments, framing issues, agenda‑setting.

## 5. Structural vs. interpersonal tactics
- **Structural**: secret ballot, open voting, framing the deliberation as a shared mission.  
- **Interpersonal**: foot‑in‑door entry, reciprocity gifts, unity appeals, direct challenges to witness credibility.

## 6. Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Secret ballot | Private written vote that reduces peer pressure. |
| Foot‑in‑door | Compliance technique starting with a small request. |
| Reciprocity | Obligation to return a favor after receiving one. |
| Unity principle | Persuasion boost when individuals identify with a common group. |
| Reasonable doubt | Standard requiring certainty before conviction. |
| Open ballot | Publicly announced votes during deliberation. |
| Framing | Presenting information to shape perception and decision‑making. |
