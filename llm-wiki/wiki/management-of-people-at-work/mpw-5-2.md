> [!summary] Key takeaways  
- Exam = 14 % of grade; 2 h, closed‑book, 50 % MCQ, 50 % essay.  
- Master 1‑2 core concepts per question; answer with problem‑analysis‑recommendation flow.  
- Essays use three‑paragraph structure: define problem, diagnose with deep theory, give evidence‑based actions.  
- Group project follows same flow; presentation 10‑12 min + Q&A.  
- Relevance > quantity; complementary theories can add credit.

## Final Exam Overview
- **Weight**: ≈14 % of course grade; 2 h, online locked eLearn, laptop only.  
- **Sections (each 50 %)**:  
  - **MCQs** – 25 items, 2 pts each → 50 marks.  
  - **Essays** – 5 items, 10 pts each → 50 marks.  
- **MCQ focus**: definitions, distinctions, scenario‑based concept application.  
- **Essay focus**: diagnosis, explanation, recommendation; format varies.

> [!info] **Exam strategy**  
Prioritise depth: master one or two key concepts per question, apply them thoroughly; relevance beats quantity.

> [!warning] Classroom activities (e.g., cosmetic‑wearer ranking) will **not** be examined.

## Answer Blueprint (MCQ & Essay)

### MCQ Tactics
- Read stem carefully; pinpoint the concept being tested.  
- Eliminate clearly wrong options; select the most precise definition or distinction.  
- For scenario items, map the situation to the relevant theory.

### Essay Structure (mirrors group project)

<div class="dc-cols"><div class="dc-card"><b>Paragraph 1</b> Problem definition <span class="dc-chip">identify</span></div><div class="dc-card"><b>Paragraph 2</b> Analysis/diagnosis <span class="dc-chip">theory</span></div><div class="dc-card"><b>Paragraph 3</b> Recommendations <span class="dc-chip">action</span></div></div>

> [!info] **Problem definition**  
State current evidence, desired state, and the gap (people problem) in 1‑2 sentences; specify who is affected and observable symptoms.

> [!info] **Analysis**  
- Choose 1‑2 highly relevant theories; avoid superficial coverage.  
- Present alternative explanations, then accept/reject logically.  
- Use public data (surveys, media) if no interview contacts.

> [!info] **Recommendations**  
- Derive concrete actions, assign responsibility.  
- Ensure feasibility given authority, resources, constraints.  
- Note conditions for success and limitations.

## Group Project Essentials

| Phase | Goal | Tips |
|------|------|------|
| Problem specification | Identify symptom vs underlying issue | Gap statement concise |
| Diagnosis | Apply narrow, deep theory | Show alternatives |
| Recommendations | Actionable, evidence‑based | Highlight feasibility & limits |

> [!warning] Over‑loading with many concepts reduces depth and may lower marks.

> [!example] **Sample answer flow**  
1. *Problem*: “Employee turnover has risen 15 % (evidence) vs target 5 % (desired). Gap = 10 % excess turnover affecting frontline staff.”  
2. *Analysis*: Apply **Job‑Characteristics Model** and **Social Exchange Theory**; reject “poor pay” as sole cause after data review.  
3. *Recommendations*: Implement job‑enrichment workshops (HR), introduce peer‑recognition program (Managers), pilot for 6 months; monitor turnover monthly.

## Presentation Guidelines
- Submit as slide deck; optional video for extra polish.  
- Duration: 10–12 min speaking, total slot ≈15 min with Q&A.  
- Prepare visual aids (pictures, charts) beforehand.  
- Coordinate timing when multiple groups present.  
- Q&A: brief but demonstrate mastery; anticipate likely questions.

## Practice & Resources
- Review provided practice MCQs and essay prompts.  
- Primary study material: concise notes summarising lecture slides.  
- Textbook optional for deeper essay content, not required.  
- Consistent referencing expected; minor formatting errors harmless.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| People problem | Gap between current evidence and desired state, affecting individuals/groups |
| Diagnosis | Narrow, deep analysis using 1‑2 relevant theories |
| Evidence‑based recommendation | Action derived from analysis, feasible within organizational constraints |
| MCQ | Multiple‑choice question testing definition, distinction, scenario application |
| Essay | Open‑text response requiring problem definition, analysis, and actionable advice |
