> [!summary] Key takeaways  
- Prepare: BATNA, reservation price, target price, market data.  
- Make a high‑but‑credible first offer to anchor the discussion.  
- Watch for four common traps: money left on table, winner’s curse, walking away, worse‑than‑status‑quo.  
- Distributive deals need a clear “pie” estimate; collaborative deals seek joint value.  
- Long‑term reputation often outweighs short‑term gains.

## Why negotiation matters & common traps
- Most people avoid negotiation or settle for symbolic concessions.  
- Executives often over‑estimate their skill due to lack of counter‑factuals.  

> [!info] **Four common traps**  
| Trap | What happens | Example |
|------|--------------|---------|
| Leaving money on the table | Fail to uncover true interests → lose‑lose | Sisters split orange half‑and‑half, missing peel vs. pulp needs |
| Winner’s Curse (settling for too little) | Accept first offer, miss better deal | Set “stretch goals” to avoid |
| Walking away (ego‑driven) | Reject beneficial offer, later regret | CEO rejected $6 B for 25 % stake → bank collapse |
| Worse than status‑quo | Continue negotiating despite inferior terms | Sunk‑cost bias, lack of market knowledge |

> [!warning] **Avoid**: letting pride or lack of data drive you away from a viable deal.

## Distributive vs. collaborative negotiation
> [!info] **Distributive negotiation** – single‑issue (price) zero‑sum; any gain for one side is a loss for the other.  
- First estimate the total “pie” before dividing.  
- Win‑win (collaborative) seeks to expand the pie by addressing underlying interests.

## Preparation checklist
> [!info] **Key preparation elements**  
- **BATNA** – your best alternative; source of leverage.  
- **Reservation price** – minimum acceptable; keep private.  
- **Target price** – desired outcome, set slightly above opponent’s reservation.  
- **Market data** – benchmarks, competitor offers, industry standards.  

> [!example] **BATNA in action** – an external job offer gave leverage in salary talks with SMU.

### Reservation vs. Target illustration
| Role | Target | Reservation | BATNA |
|------|--------|-------------|-------|
| Buyer | $150 M | $100 M (status‑quo) | Alternative supplier at $120 M |
| Seller | $200 M | $160 M (cost base) | Keep asset, no sale |

- Over‑ambitious targets trigger the “winner’s curse.”  
- A viable deal requires overlap between buyer’s max willingness and seller’s reservation.

## First offer & anchoring
> [!info] **Anchoring rule** – the first offer sets a reference point that pulls the final price toward it.  
- Start **high but credible**; support with industry benchmarks.  
- Concessions feel rewarding to the counterpart; begin high to retain room.  

> [!warning] **Don’t** make an unrealistically extreme offer that destroys credibility.

### When to let the other side go first
- If you lack reliable market data, BATNA, or knowledge of the counterpart’s constraints.  
- After their offer: acknowledge, then quickly re‑anchor with your prepared number and ask diagnostic questions.

## In‑class exercise insights
- **Setup**: 1‑v‑1 COO role‑play, Energetics vs. Generics, cash‑only deal, 15‑min negotiation.  
- **Key observations**  
  - Prices ranged wildly (e.g., $400 M vs. $189 k) despite identical information.  
  - Groups that aimed for win‑win reported more collaborative language.  
  - Sellers often quoted highest numbers; buyers used cash‑flow urgency and small incentives ($15 M) to justify higher offers.  
  - First offers proved decisive (high anchor → higher final price).  
  - Long‑term relationship considerations favor less aggressive tactics; repeated interactions change strategy.  

> [!example] **Group 50** – buyer anchored at $189 k, lowered to $150 k, then met in the middle; first offer shaped outcome.

## Strategic tips
- **Mind‑reading**: infer counterpart’s true preferences; frame proposals from their perspective.  
- **Collaborative atmosphere**: use neutral language (“Grace”) to signal joint problem‑solving.  
- **Confidence**: present offers firmly, back them with data, but avoid over‑explaining BATNA details.  
- **Avoid over‑ambitious targets** to prevent dissatisfaction after the deal.  

## Common cautions (recap)
> [!warning] **Winner’s Curse** – setting targets too high leads to feeling short‑changed.  
> [!warning] **Leaving money on the table** – ignore hidden interests, lose value.  
> [!warning] **Walking away** – pride can cost you a good deal.  
> [!warning] **Worse‑than‑status‑quo** – keep negotiating only if terms improve on the baseline.

## Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| BATNA | Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement; your leverage source |
| Reservation price | Minimum acceptable outcome; never disclose |
| Target price | Desired outcome, set above opponent’s reservation |
| Anchoring | Influence of the first offer on the perceived value |
| Winner’s Curse | Accepting a deal that feels unsatisfactory because expectations were unrealistic |
| Distributive negotiation | Zero‑sum, single‑issue bargaining |
| Collaborative (win‑win) | Expanding the pie by addressing underlying interests |
