> [!summary] Key takeaways  
- Defaults and framing dramatically shift choices; defaults can raise consent from ~20 % to ~90 %.  
- A **group** lacks interdependence and synergy; a **team** has both and can outperform the sum of its members.  
- Team performance hinges on **composition**, **processes/states**, and **context**.  
- Bias‑mitigation tools include nudges, incentives, and pre‑mortem analysis.  
- In the desert‑survival task, each member ranks items individually, then the team must agree on a single combined ranking within 50 minutes.

## Decision‑making biases
- **Defaults**: Opt‑out organ‑donation in Singapore lifts consent from ~20‑30 % to ~80‑90 % → illustrates power of default settings.  
- **Framing effect**: Question wording changes responses (e.g., “how happy?” vs. “how unhappy?”).  
- **Bounded/founded rationality**: Decisions are made with limited info and cognitive capacity.  
- **Selection & survivorship bias**: Use representative samples; WWII plane‑repair case missed damage on planes that never returned.  
- **Sunk‑cost fallacy**: Ignore past investments; evaluate only future expected outcomes.  
- **Overconfidence / better‑than‑average**: People routinely rate themselves above the median.  
- **Power distance**: Low power distance aids rapid crisis coordination; high distance hinders information flow.  

> [!warning] **Avoid** basing decisions on non‑representative data or on past sunk costs; these introduce systematic errors.

## Group vs. Team
> [!info] **Group** – ≥2 individuals who interact, are interdependent, and share a common objective.  
> [!info] **Work group** – members share info, work separately, accountability is individual; output ≈ sum of parts.  
> [!info] **Work team** – interdependent members, shared objective, positive synergy (output > sum); accountability to the team.

| Criterion            | Group / Work group | Team / Work team |
|----------------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Interdependence      | Low / none        | High            |
| Shared objective     | Weak / optional   | Strong, explicit|
| Synergy              | Neutral/negative  | Positive (> sum)|
| Accountability       | Individual        | Collective      |

- **Audience at a concert** ≠ group/team (no coordinated goal).  
- **Car‑dealership floor** ≈ work group; occasional lead hand‑off = brief team‑like interaction.

## Team types & effectiveness
- **Four common team types**  
  1. **Problem‑solving** – tackle a specific issue.  
  2. **Self‑managed** – self‑directed, interdependent.  
  3. **Cross‑functional** – blend functions for a project.  
  4. **Virtual** – geographically dispersed, tech‑mediated.  

- **Three pillars of effectiveness**  
  1. **Composition** – diversity (personality, culture, function), roles, size.  
  2. **Processes & states** – conflict management, cohesion, motivation, psychological safety, transactive memory.  
  3. **Context** – resources, leadership, structure, culture, incentives, technology, crisis conditions.  

> [!info] **Assess any team** by asking: *Composition? Processes/States? Context?*

## Bias‑mitigation interventions
- **Financial incentives / nudges** → steer healthier or safer choices.  
- **System design** → prompts, training modules, serious‑games.  
- **Pre‑mortem** → imagine failures, work backward to preventive actions.  

## Desert‑survival ranking exercise (worked example)
> [!example] **Steps**  
1. **Individual ranking** – each student orders 15 salvaged items (1 = most essential).  
2. **Team ranking** – group has 50 minutes to reach consensus; no changes to personal lists after start.  
3. **Submission** – all members record the agreed team list; instructor gives a final 5‑minute warning.  

- **Items discussed**: rain/water, air map, compass, cosmetic mirrors, flashlights, temperature‑management code, pistol.  
- **Key debate**: rain needed only for shelter vs. essential for survival.  
- **Rule**: once the ranking phase begins, **no edits** to individual lists; the team must submit a single unified ranking.

## Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Default effect | Influence of pre‑set options on choice frequency |
| Framing effect | Change in response caused by wording of a question |
| Bounded rationality | Decision making with limited information/capacity |
| Power distance | Degree to which less‑powerful members expect unequal power distribution |
| Psychological safety | Shared belief that the team is safe for interpersonal risk‑taking |
| Transactive memory | Collective awareness of who holds which expertise |
| Pre‑mortem | Structured imagination of future failure to identify preventive steps |
