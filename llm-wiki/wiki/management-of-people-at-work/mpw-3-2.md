> [!summary] Key takeaways  
- Motivation = intensity + direction + persistence; it drives effort.  
- Expectancy Theory: high expectancy + instrumentality + valence → strong motivation.  
- Fair, transparent rewards (justice) link performance to personal goals.  
- Dealership case: Jason’s clear self‑set goals → high effort; Bobby’s mis‑aligned leads → low performance.  
- Tiered, meaningful rewards outperform all‑or‑nothing bonuses.

## 1. Core Motivation Concepts

> [!info] **Motivation dimensions**  
- **Intensity** – amount of effort.  
- **Direction** – effort applied to the right tasks.  
- **Persistence** – duration of effort.

> [!info] **Performance prerequisites**  
| Prerequisite | Role |
|---|---|
| Ability | Skills/knowledge |
| Motivation | Effort (intensity + direction + persistence) |
| Opportunity/Resources | Tools, health, time, support |

## 2. Expectancy Theory Framework

> [!info] **Expectancy** – belief that effort improves performance.  
> [!info] **Instrumentality** – belief that performance yields desired outcomes.  
> [!info] **Valence** – personal value of those outcomes.

> [!example] **Motivation strength** = high expectancy + high instrumentality + high valence → strong drive; any low component weakens motivation.

## 3. Organizational Justice Dimensions

> [!info] **Four justice types**  

| Type | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Distributive | Fairness of outcome distribution |
| Procedural | Fairness of decision processes |
| Interactional | Respectful treatment & voice |
| Informational | Honesty & transparency |

> [!warning] Over‑reliance on external validation (e.g., constant bonuses) can erode intrinsic drive and increase burnout.

## 4. Dealership Reward Structure

> [!info] **Current system** (all‑or‑nothing)  

| Level | Target | Bonus |
|---|---|---|
| Dealership | 129 cars (Oct) | $65‑$85 k |
| Salesperson | 15‑20 cars | 20 % commission; ≥target → 30 % |

> [!warning] All‑or‑nothing bonuses create unstable instrumentality; a single missed car eliminates reward.

> [!example] **Tiered alternative** – incremental commission bumps at 10, 15, 20 cars provide steady instrumentality.

## 5. Case Study: Motivation in Action

### 5.1 Jason (high performer)

- **Goal**: 30 cars/month (stretch → 40).  
- **Drivers**: self‑competition, achievement, status.  
- **Key traits**: *constructive delusion* (100 % optimism) & *intense personal drive*.  
- **Effort alignment**: clear personal goal → high intensity, right direction, sustained persistence.  
- **Reward perception**: commissions reinforce but personal achievement is primary valence.

### 5.2 Bobby T. (low performer)

- **Goal**: 15 cars/month (standard).  
- **Barriers**: poor lead allocation, unclear cues, perceived unfairness.  
- **Effort misdirection**: high effort but wrong direction (bad leads).  
- **Motivation gaps**: low expectancy (effort → performance unclear) & weak instrumentality (rewards feel unjust).  
- **Desired reward**: higher commission & status, but uncertainty undermines motivation.

> [!example] **Effort‑Performance link** – Jason’s effort translates directly into sales; Bobby’s effort is diluted by lead mishandling and ambiguous expectations.

## 6. Diagnosing Motivation Problems

1. **Check expectancy** – Do employees believe effort improves performance?  
2. **Check instrumentality** – Is performance reliably linked to valued rewards?  
3. **Check valence** – Do rewards match personal goals?  
4. **Assess justice** – Are outcomes, processes, interactions, and information perceived as fair?

## 7. Recommendations for the Dealership

- **Introduce tiered commissions** to stabilize instrumentality.  
- **Clarify lead distribution** and give salespeople autonomy over follow‑up (boost expectancy).  
- **Communicate targets and reward formulas transparently** (informational justice).  
- **Solicit employee input** on reward design (interactional justice).  
- **Align bonuses with personal goals** (e.g., “top‑seller” recognition) to increase valence.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Expectancy Theory | Model linking effort → performance → reward via expectancy, instrumentality, valence |
| Motivation dimensions | Intensity, direction, persistence of effort |
| Organizational justice | Fairness across distributive, procedural, interactional, informational domains |
| Instrumentality | Belief that performance will lead to desired outcomes |
| Valence | Personal value placed on the reward |
