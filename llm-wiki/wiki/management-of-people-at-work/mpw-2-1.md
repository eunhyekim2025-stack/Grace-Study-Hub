## Why personality in an OB class

Managing people means predicting how they will behave — and personality is one input. But it is an **approximation**: useful for anticipating tendencies (who fits a role, where friction may appear in a team), not a verdict on a person.

## MBTI (Myers-Briggs) — popular ≠ scientific

The most popular personality test in the world, and **broadly disliked by personality scientists**. It sorts you into 1 of 16 types across four binaries (e.g. INTJ, ESFJ).

| Why people love it | Why scientists reject it |
| --- | --- |
| Recognizable, simple, memorable | **Unreliable** — ~50% get a *different* type when they retake it weeks later |
| Non-judgmental — every type is flattering | **Invalid** — it does not predict meaningful work/life outcomes |
| Helps people recognize individual differences | **Forces binaries** — traits are really *continuous* (a normal distribution); it splits people who are high on both sides |
| Good business model (free type + paid report) | **Pigeonholes** — typing people invites stereotyping |

> [!info] Why a flattering test *feels* accurate
> Every MBTI description is positively valenced, so you tend to agree with it — the same reason a horoscope or fortune-teller "sounds right." Good-sounding feedback is easy to accept as true (a Barnum/Forer effect).

## What personality is

- **Enduring traits** — not quite present at birth; they *crystallize in adolescence* and then stay fairly stable.
- **Maturation effect** — with age we tend to become *more conscientious*, *more agreeable* (contrary to the "grumpy old person" stereotype), and often more politically conservative. So personality can shift subtly later in life.
- Organizations therefore use **psychometric testing** in hiring — but combined with other sources (assessment centres, structured/unstructured interviews, other-ratings), never a single test in isolation.

## The Big Five — the Five-Factor Model (OCEAN)

The most widely studied, scientifically-backed personality framework. Mnemonic: **OCEAN**. Each dimension is a *continuum*, not a category.

| Trait | High scorer | Workplace signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **O**penness | Curious, creative, appreciates novelty & variety | Higher performance **during change**; very high can mean impulsivity/sensation-seeking |
| **C**onscientiousness | Disciplined, dutiful, achievement-striving, regulates impulses | **Strongest Big Five predictor of job performance**; adapts to changing task demands; extreme → perfectionism / burnout |
| **E**xtraversion | Energized by social, stimulating settings; talkative | Strong predictor of **leadership *emergence*** (who gets the role — not effectiveness); orgs reward it; extraverts hit harder by remote work |
| **A**greeableness | Values harmony, cooperation, helping others | Higher performance in **service/interpersonal** jobs; but linked to **lower earnings & career success**, and *negatively* associated with leadership emergence |
| **N**euroticism *(low = emotional stability)* | Prone to anxiety, low stress tolerance | More stress/burnout, lower job satisfaction; upside: **vigilance to threats** (useful in risk/health-&-safety roles) |

> [!quote] On agreeableness and leadership (George Bernard Shaw)
> "The reasonable man adapts himself to the world; the unreasonable one persists in trying to adapt the world to himself. Therefore all progress depends on the unreasonable man." Being *too* agreeable means accepting things as they are — but leadership often requires the opposite: insisting something can be better.

> [!warning] The Big Five is still reductionistic
> Five numbers do not capture a whole person. Two people with identical scores are not the same person, and a 67-vs-68 on extraversion means almost nothing. Treat scores as **approximations that predict averages**, not a full portrait.

## Who judges your personality best?

- **Other people are more accurate** on *visible* traits — conscientiousness, extraversion.
- **You are more accurate** on *internal/private* traits — emotional stability (little external manifestation).
- **Best = combine both** (self-report + other-ratings), which is why organizations diversify the source.

## Personality is partly readable — and partly inherited

- **Digital footprint:** algorithms predict personality (and orientation, intelligence, substance use) from Facebook "likes" — ~10 likes beat a colleague, 150 beat a family member, 300 beat a spouse.
- **Heritability:** twin studies (the "twins separated at birth" cases) suggest personality is roughly **40–60% heritable** — a real basis, but far from the whole story.

## Person × Situation — the core OB idea

Behaviour is **not** person *or* situation; it is how the two **fit**.

- **Fundamental Attribution Error (FAE):** we overweight a person's disposition and underweight their situation when judging *others* ("that driver is a bad person," not "probably late"). For *ourselves* we do the reverse — **self-serving bias** (blame the situation).
- **Fit is the real predictor** — person–job, person–organization, person–group, person–supervisor. ("People quit their manager, not their job.")
- **Situational strength theory:** in *weak/ambiguous* situations (no clear norms — e.g. a brand-new team) personality shows through; *strong* situations (clear expected conduct) suppress it.
- **Trait activation theory:** situational **cues** make a trait *relevant*, which draws out its behaviour — a socially competitive task activates extraversion, so it predicts performance there.

> [!note] Final word
> Traits make a role *easier or harder*, but they are **not destiny**. They create tendencies; fit and situation decide the outcome case by case.

## Key takeaways

- **MBTI** is popular but **unreliable and invalid** — fun, not predictive; it forces continuous traits into binaries and invites stereotyping.
- The **Big Five (OCEAN)** is the scientific model; **conscientiousness** best predicts job performance; **extraversion** predicts leadership *emergence* (not effectiveness).
- Personality is a **reductionistic approximation** (~40–60% heritable) — it predicts tendencies, not the whole person.
- Behaviour = **person × situation → fit**; beware the **fundamental attribution error**; strong situations mute personality (**situational strength**), and cues **activate** relevant traits.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Reliability / validity** | Is a test *consistent* (same result on retest) / does it *predict* what it claims? MBTI fails both |
| **Big Five (OCEAN)** | Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism |
| **Leadership emergence** | Who *becomes* a leader — distinct from leadership *effectiveness* |
| **Fundamental attribution error** | Overweighting disposition, underweighting situation, when judging others |
| **Situational strength** | Strong situations constrain behaviour and hide personality; weak ones reveal it |
| **Trait activation** | Situational cues make a trait relevant, eliciting its behavioural expression |
| **Person–organization fit** | Match between an individual's traits and the job/org/team/supervisor |
